# q5: Among players with at least one NBA championship, what is the most championships won by any player of each nationality?
## Query contract
**Natural language:** Among players with at least one NBA championship, what is the most championships won by any player of each nationality?

**Reference SQL**
```sql
SELECT nationality, MAX(nba_championships) AS max_championships FROM player WHERE nba_championships >= 1 AND nationality != '' GROUP BY nationality
```

**QuWARTS compiled SQL**
```sql
SELECT nationality, MAX(nba_championships) AS max_championships FROM player WHERE nba_championships >= 1 AND nationality != '' GROUP BY nationality
```

The SQL contracts are equivalent unless the two SQL blocks visibly differ. Therefore, documented failures below are downstream of intent/compilation.

### Why QuWARTS differs
- **Pipeline stage:** Meaning of title counts
- **Root cause:** QuWARTS retained wrong NBA title counts, including a value of 6 linked to a hockey player's Stanley Cups.
- **Pipeline behavior behind it:** The selected working table checks that this field contains a number. It does not apply an NBA-specific meaning or range check.
- **Why validation allowed it:** A count of six fits the numeric type even when it counts titles from another sport.
- **How it affected the result:** Wrong title counts became the highest value for some nationalities. Different nationality names created more group errors. One error also comes from an unusual empty value in the scoring data.

**What I found in the final and working tables**
- Dominican and Nigerian-American are replaced by Dominican Republic and Nigerian; Yugoslav is an additional unmatched group.
- Canadian is reported as 6 rather than 1, indicating a wrong championship count on at least one retained record.
- The saved validation records link the Canadian value of 6 to Larry Robinson. His benchmark NBA title count is 0, while the article describes six Stanley Cups.
- The Player CSV contains Steve Kerr with 9 NBA championships, but the saved gold result for American is an empty string. Empty numeric text passes the SQLite comparison and is selected by MAX, so this part of the score is a benchmark data-type edge case. QuWARTS reports 6, which is also below the source-table maximum of 9.

### Why DocETL differs
- **Pipeline stage:** Missing rows, names, and a scoring edge case
- **Root cause:** The DocETL output omitted 25 of the 141 input player documents and split nationality names. Its American value of 9 matches Steve Kerr's source row, but the benchmark result for that group is an empty string because of mixed text and number handling.
- **Pipeline behavior behind it:** The map step is configured to skip a document after an extraction error, and it uses one free-text nationality field with no shared mapping. The saved output does not record the reason for each omitted document.
- **Why validation allowed it:** The output checks cannot recover a skipped player or join two nationality spellings. The score also treats the empty benchmark value as different from the valid number 9.
- **How it affected the result:** Omitted or incomplete rows removed groups, name differences added other groups, and the benchmark edge case marks the American value as wrong even though 9 is present in the source table.

**What I found in the final and working tables**
- The intermediate player table contains only 116 rows. It has 65 empty nationalities and 81 title counts marked as missing. The at-least-one rule removes those missing title counts from the final result.
- The American maximum is 9, which matches Steve Kerr's row in the Player CSV. It is marked wrong only because the saved gold result is the empty-string SQLite edge case. Nigerian-American is 2 instead of 1.
- All 141 player documents were sent to the map step, but OG Anunoby and Giannis Antetokounmpo are absent from its 116-row output. The map operation is configured to skip a document after an extraction error.
- The gold labels "British" and "Greek and Nigerian" are missing. Croatian, Dutch, Greek, and Slovenian are extra groups.
