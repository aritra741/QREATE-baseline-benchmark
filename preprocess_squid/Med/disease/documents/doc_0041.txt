Once upon a time, there was a condition known as Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS), which could be idiopathic, inflammatory, psychiatric, or others. The exact cause of IBS was often unknown but might also stem from a gastrointestinal infection, stress, anger, microbial imbalance, or endocannabinoid deficiency. Doctors would typically diagnose the condition through clinical evaluation.

The common symptoms included nausea, abdominal pain, cramping, gas, bloating, constipation, diarrhea, weight loss, and even rectal bleeding. Treatments for IBS often involved a combination of oral medication, lifestyle modifications, dietary interventions, or other approaches. The prognosis for those with IBS was generally considered a chronic condition that could significantly impact the quality of life; however, its severity varied from person to person.

The pathogenesis of IBS could also be idiopathic, inflammatory, or other. One of the main challenges in treating IBS included poor efficacy of medications, serious side effects, and a lack of solid clinical evidence to guide treatment decisions.
