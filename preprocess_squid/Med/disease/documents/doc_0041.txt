Once upon a time, someone was diagnosed with Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS), a condition that can be idiopathic, inflammatory, psychiatric, or related to other factors. The exact cause of IBS often remains unknown but can also result from gastrointestinal infections, stress, anger, microbial imbalances, or endocannabinoid deficiencies.

The diagnosis is usually based on clinical evaluation, and common symptoms include nausea, abdominal pain, cramping, gas, bloating, constipation, diarrhea, weight loss, and rectal bleeding. Treatments might involve oral medication, lifestyle modifications, dietary interventions, or a combination of these approaches. The prognosis for IBS can vary; it's often considered a chronic condition that can significantly impact the quality of life. However, its pathogenesis is complex and can be idiopathic, inflammatory, or related to other factors.

Moreover, there are challenges in treating IBS due to poor efficacy of certain medications, serious side effects, and limited clinical evidence supporting some treatment options.
