Once upon a time, someone was diagnosed with Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS), which can be idiopathic, inflammatory, or psychiatric in nature. The exact cause of IBS was unknown, but it could also result from gastrointestinal infections, stress, anger, microbial imbalances, or endocannabinoid deficiency. To diagnose the condition, doctors relied on clinical evaluation. Common symptoms included nausea, abdominal pain, cramping, gas, bloating, constipation, diarrhea, weight loss, and rectal bleeding. Treatment options ranged from oral medication and lifestyle modifications to dietary interventions and other approaches. The prognosis for IBS was often described as a chronic condition that could significantly impair quality of life, though the outlook varied widely. Pathogenesis could be idiopathic, inflammatory, or via other mechanisms. Notably, treatment challenges included poor efficacy, serious side effects, and a lack of robust clinical evidence.
