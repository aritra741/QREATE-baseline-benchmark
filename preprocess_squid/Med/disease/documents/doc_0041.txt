Once upon a time, there was a person who was diagnosed with Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS). The disease type could be idiopathic, inflammatory, or psychiatric, and the etiology might stem from unknown causes, gastrointestinal infections, stress, anger, microbial imbalances, or even an endocannabinoid deficiency. To diagnose it, doctors relied on clinical evaluations. Common symptoms included nausea, abdominal pain, cramping, gas, bloating, constipation, diarrhea, weight loss, and rectal bleeding.

For treatment, options ranged from oral medications to lifestyle modifications and dietary interventions, with some patients seeking other treatments as well. The prognosis for IBS was often a chronic condition that significantly impacted the quality of life or varied greatly among individuals. Pathogenesis could also be idiopathic, inflammatory, or involve other factors. Treatment challenges included poor efficacy, serious side effects, and a lack of clinical evidence to support certain approaches.
