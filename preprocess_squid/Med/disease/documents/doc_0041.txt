So, let's talk about Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS). It can be idiopathic, inflammatory, or psychiatric in nature, and its exact cause remains unknown but could also stem from a gastrointestinal infection, stress, anger, microbial imbalance, or even endocannabinoid deficiency. Diagnosis is typically based on clinical evaluation. Common symptoms include nausea, abdominal pain, cramping, gas, bloating, constipation, diarrhea, weight loss, and rectal bleeding. Treatments often involve oral medication, lifestyle modifications, dietary interventions, or a combination of these approaches. The prognosis for IBS can be chronic, leading to quality-of-life impairment, but the condition's severity varies widely among individuals. Pathogenesis can be idiopathic, inflammatory, or other factors. Treatment challenges include poor efficacy, serious side effects, and limited clinical evidence.
