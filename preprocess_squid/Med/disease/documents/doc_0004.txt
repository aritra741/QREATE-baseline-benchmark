Once upon a time, there was a person living with Diabetes type 1. This condition falls under the categories of autoimmune and endocrine diseases, influenced by genetic susceptibility, environmental factors, diet, and infections. To diagnose it, doctors rely on laboratory tests. Common symptoms included thirst, frequent urination, weight loss despite a healthy appetite, feelings of weakness, muscle pain, fatigue, irritability, nausea, vomiting, and blurry vision. Treatment often involved injections coupled with dietary interventions and lifestyle modifications. While Diabetes type 1 is considered a chronic condition that can impact the quality of life, there are challenges in managing it effectively.
