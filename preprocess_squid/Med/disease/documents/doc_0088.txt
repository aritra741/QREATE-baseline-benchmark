The disease name is Sarcopenia, which falls under the categories of degenerative, nutritional, endocrine, and inflammatory. Its causes include a lack of physical activity, poor diet, questionable lifestyle vices, systemic inflammatory disorders, decreased growth hormone levels, and significant underlying medical problems. For diagnosis, clinicians rely on clinical evaluation, functional tests, and imaging techniques. Common symptoms include a decline in muscle mass and strength, reduced function, increased risk of bone fractures, difficulty standing or walking, body pains, balance issues, shortened life span, fear of injury, social isolation, avoidance behaviors, anxiety, depression, challenges with self-care, difficulties driving, shopping, bathing, dressing, and completing household chores. Treatments often involve physical therapy, lifestyle modifications, dietary interventions, and oral medications. The prognosis can vary from partial recovery to a chronic condition that may be progressive, leading to quality of life impairment.
