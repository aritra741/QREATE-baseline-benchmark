One day, a person might notice they're feeling weaker and more fatigued. They start to experience a decline in muscle mass and strength, which makes it harder for them to do everyday tasks like standing up or walking. These symptoms can be caused by a lack of physical activity, poor diet, questionable lifestyle choices, systemic inflammatory disorders, decreased growth hormone levels, or significant underlying medical problems. Doctors might use clinical evaluations, functional tests, and imaging techniques to diagnose the condition known as sarcopenia.

Common signs include difficulty standing up from a chair, trouble walking, body pains, balance issues, and an overall decline in quality of life. This can lead to fears of injury, social isolation, anxiety, depression, and difficulties with self-care activities like bathing or dressing. The treatments for sarcopenia often involve physical therapy, lifestyle modifications, dietary interventions, and sometimes oral medications.

While some people might experience partial recovery, others could face a chronic condition that progressively worsens, impacting their overall quality of life. The pathogenesis of sarcopenia is complex, involving degenerative processes, nutritional deficiencies, endocrine disorders, and inflammatory responses.
