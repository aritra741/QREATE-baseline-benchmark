Once upon a time, someone was dealing with a condition called Sinus Migraine. This condition fell under the categories of inflammatory and idiopathic causes, though the exact reasons weren't fully understood. To diagnose it, doctors relied mainly on clinical evaluations. Common symptoms included nasal congestion, tearing of the eyes, runny nose, ear pain, ear pressure, dizziness, head pain, nausea, light sensitivity, sound sensitivity, smell sensitivity, a pressing sensation across the forehead, and pains behind the eyes and beneath the cheeks.

For treatment, oral medications were often used. However, there were challenges in managing this condition as it sometimes had a poor response to standard sinusitis treatments.
