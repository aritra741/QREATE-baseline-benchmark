Once upon a time, someone was diagnosed with Sinus Migraine. This condition can be either inflammatory, idiopathic, or related to other factors. The doctor relied on clinical evaluation for diagnosis since the exact cause (etiology) and specific diagnostic methods weren't clear. Common symptoms included nasal congestion, tearing of the eyes, runny nose, ear pain, ear pressure, dizziness, head pain, nausea, light sensitivity, sound sensitivity, smell sensitivity, pressure across the forehead, pain behind the eyes, pain behind the nose, and pain beneath the cheeks. For treatment, oral medication was often used, but there were challenges as patients sometimes didn't respond well to standard sinusitis treatments.
