Once upon a time, there was a condition called Thoracic Outlet Syndrome (TOS), which could be traumatic, vascular, congenital, or from other causes. It often started with an injury in an automobile or motorcycle accident, repetitive stress injuries from sports or employment, pregnancy, or anatomical differences like having an extra rib. To diagnose TOS, doctors typically relied on a clinical evaluation. Common symptoms included muscle wasting in the hands, numbness and tingling in the arms and fingers, aches and pains in the shoulder area, arm pain and swelling, and weakening grip and arm strength. Treatments ranged from physical therapy to oral medication or even surgery. Since TOS was often a chronic condition, managing it required a long-term approach. The exact pathogenesis could be vascular, traumatic, congenital, or other depending on the case.
