Once upon a time, someone was dealing with Opioid-Induced Constipation, which is an iatrogenic condition caused by opioid use. To diagnose it, doctors would rely on clinical evaluation. Common symptoms included difficult defecation, painful defecation, incomplete bowel-emptying, decreased frequency of emptying, hard and lumpy stools, and straining. Treatments often involved lifestyle modification, oral medication, and dietary intervention. The prognosis was variable, but challenges could arise if first and second line laxative treatments failed, sometimes requiring specialist medications to get relief.
