Once upon a time, someone was dealing with Opioid-Induced Constipation, which is an iatrogenic condition caused by the use of opioids. The doctors diagnosed it through clinical evaluation. Common symptoms included difficult defecation, painful defecation, incomplete bowel-emptying, decreased frequency of emptying, hard and lumpy stools, and straining. Treatments often involved lifestyle modifications, oral medications, and dietary interventions. The prognosis varied from person to person. However, treatment could be challenging if first- and second-line laxative treatments failed, sometimes requiring specialist medications.
