Once upon a time, someone was dealing with Opioid-Induced Constipation, which is an iatrogenic condition caused by opioid use. To diagnose it, doctors rely on clinical evaluation. Common symptoms include difficult defecation, painful defecation, incomplete bowel-emptying, decreased frequency of emptying, hard and lumpy stools, and straining. Treatments often involve lifestyle modification, oral medication, and dietary intervention. The prognosis can be quite variable, and there are challenges in treatment, such as the failure of first and second-line laxative treatments and a need for specialist medications.
