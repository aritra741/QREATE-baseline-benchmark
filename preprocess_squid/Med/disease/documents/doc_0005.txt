Once upon a time, there was a genetic condition called Vohwinkel syndrome. This disease happens because of mutations in the GJB2 gene, and doctors diagnose it through clinical evaluations. People with this syndrome often experience a variety of symptoms including loss of fingers and toes, starfish-shaped patches of thickened skin, honeycomb-like calluses on their palms and soles, and patches of infected skin. They might also feel itching and experience peeling skin. Treatments can involve oral medications or surgery, but the prognosis isn't always positive; many face disability and quality-of-life impairment, though in some cases, it remains stable.
