Once upon a time, there was Vohwinkel syndrome, a genetic condition caused by mutations in the GJB2 gene. Doctors diagnose it through clinical evaluation and look out for symptoms like loss of fingers, toes, starfish-shaped patches of thickened skin, honeycomb-like calluses on palms and soles, and patches of infected skin. People with Vohwinkel syndrome might also experience itching and peeling. Treatment options include oral medication and surgery, but the prognosis can vary; it often leads to some level of disability or impairment in quality of life, though it can sometimes be stable. The exact pathogenesis involves genetic mutations, which make treatment a bit challenging at times.
