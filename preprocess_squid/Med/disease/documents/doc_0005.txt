Once upon a time, there was a condition called Vohwinkel syndrome. This disease is genetic and results from mutations in the GJB2 gene. Doctors usually diagnose it through clinical evaluation. The common symptoms include loss of fingers and toes, starfish-shaped patches of thickened skin, honeycomb-like calluses on palms and soles, patches of infected skin, itching, and peeling. Treatments often involve oral medication and surgery, but the prognosis can vary, leading to disability or quality-of-life impairment in some cases, though it can be stable for others. The root cause is genetic mutation, which poses challenges during treatment.
