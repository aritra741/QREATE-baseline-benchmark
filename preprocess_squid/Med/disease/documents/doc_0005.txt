In the story of Vohwinkel syndrome, a genetic condition caused by mutations in the GJB2 gene, doctors rely on clinical evaluation to diagnose patients who often experience symptoms like loss of fingers and toes, starfish-shaped patches of thickened skin, honeycomb-like calluses on palms and soles, and patches of infected skin. Patients might also suffer from itching and peeling skin. Treatment options include oral medication and surgery, though the prognosis can lead to disability and impairment in quality of life, with some cases remaining stable. The underlying cause is due to genetic mutation, which presents challenges for treatment.
