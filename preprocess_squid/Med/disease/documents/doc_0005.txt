Once upon a time, there was a condition known as Vohwinkel syndrome. It's a genetic disease that occurs due to mutations in the GJB2 gene. To diagnose it, doctors rely on clinical evaluations. People with this syndrome often experience loss of fingers and toes, along with starfish-shaped patches of thickened skin, honeycomb-like calluses on their palms and soles, and patches of infected skin. They might also feel itching or experience peeling skin. Treatment usually involves a combination of oral medication and sometimes surgery. While the condition can lead to disability and impact quality of life, it is generally stable over time. The underlying cause of these symptoms is linked to genetic mutations.
