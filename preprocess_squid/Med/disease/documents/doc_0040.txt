Once upon a time, there was a condition called Diabetes Mellitus, which falls under the categories of metabolic and endocrine diseases. The main reasons for this disease are either a lack of insulin production or insulin resistance. Doctors diagnose it through laboratory tests, and common symptoms include frequent urination (polyuria), excessive thirst (polydipsia), weight loss, increased appetite, decreased appetite, cloudy eyes, chronic infections, and recurring infections. Treatment involves injections, more laboratory tests, dietary interventions, and lifestyle modifications. While diabetes is a chronic condition that can impact the quality of life, it's important to note that it can sometimes be life-threatening. The underlying mechanisms involve metabolic disorders and endocrine issues, making its management quite complex.
