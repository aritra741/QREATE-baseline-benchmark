Once upon a time, there was a disease called Diabetes Mellitus, which falls into the categories of metabolic and endocrine diseases. This condition arises due to either a lack of insulin production or insulin resistance in the body. To diagnose it, doctors often rely on laboratory tests. Common symptoms include frequent urination (polyuria), excessive thirst (polydipsia), weight loss, increased appetite, decreased appetite, cloudy eyes, chronic infections, and recurring infections.

To manage this condition, treatments may involve injections of insulin, dietary interventions, lifestyle modifications, and even further laboratory tests. The prognosis for Diabetes Mellitus is often described as a chronic condition that can significantly impact the quality of life and in some cases, become life-threatening. Its pathogenesis involves metabolic and endocrine disorders, making its management complex.
