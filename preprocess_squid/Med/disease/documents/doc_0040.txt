Once upon a time, there was a disease called Diabetes Mellitus. It's a metabolic and endocrine disorder that often stems from the lack of insulin production or insulin resistance in the body. To diagnose it, doctors usually rely on laboratory tests. People with this condition commonly experience polyuria (frequent urination), polydipsia (increased thirst), weight loss, an increased or decreased appetite, cloudy eyes, and recurrent infections. Treatments often include injections, lifestyle modifications, dietary interventions, and more laboratory tests to monitor the condition. The prognosis of Diabetes Mellitus is generally considered a chronic condition that can significantly impact one's quality of life but could be life-threatening if not managed properly. Understanding its pathogenesis as a metabolic or endocrine disorder helps in addressing treatment challenges that often make management complex.
