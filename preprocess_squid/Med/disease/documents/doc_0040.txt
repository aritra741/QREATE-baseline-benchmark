Once upon a time, there was a disease called Diabetes Mellitus. This disease falls under the categories of metabolic and endocrine conditions. It often stems from either a lack of insulin production or insulin resistance. To diagnose it, doctors typically rely on laboratory tests. Common symptoms include frequent urination (polyuria), excessive thirst (polydipsia), weight loss despite increased appetite, and even decreased appetite along with cloudy eyes, chronic infections, and recurring infections.

Treatment for Diabetes Mellitus often involves a mix of injections, dietary interventions, lifestyle modifications, and sometimes laboratory tests to monitor progress. While it is considered a chronic condition that can impact the quality of life, in severe cases, it can also be life-threatening. The pathogenesis of this disease revolves around metabolic disorders and endocrine imbalances. Managing Diabetes Mellitus comes with its own set of challenges, making it a complex task for both patients and healthcare providers.
