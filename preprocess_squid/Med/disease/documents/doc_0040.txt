Once upon a time, there was a disease called Diabetes Mellitus, which falls under the categories of metabolic and endocrine disorders. It often arises due to either a lack of insulin production or insulin resistance. To diagnose it, doctors typically rely on laboratory tests. Common symptoms include polyuria (frequent urination), polydipsia (increased thirst), weight loss, increased appetite, decreased appetite, cloudy eyes, chronic infections, and recurring infections. Treatments often involve injections, dietary interventions, lifestyle modifications, and sometimes laboratory tests to monitor progress. While it's a chronic condition that can impact the quality of life, in severe cases, it can be life-threatening. The exact pathogenesis involves metabolic disorders and endocrine imbalances, making its management complex.
