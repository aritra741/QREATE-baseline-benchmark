Once upon a time, there was a condition known as Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (also called myalgic encephalomyelitis), which can be understood through its type—both psychiatric and infectious/viral, with some cases being idiopathic. The causes are believed to involve psychological factors, stress, and viruses similar to those found in mouse leukemia.

To diagnose this condition, doctors rely on clinical evaluation since there isn't a specific test for it. Common symptoms include fatigue and physical functioning impairment. Treatments often involve psychotherapy, physical therapy, and lifestyle modifications.

The prognosis can be quite challenging as Chronic Fatigue Syndrome is typically a chronic condition that impacts the quality of life. Treatment challenges include poor insurance coverage, the possibility that treatment might worsen symptoms, and the need to exclude severe cases before diagnosis.
