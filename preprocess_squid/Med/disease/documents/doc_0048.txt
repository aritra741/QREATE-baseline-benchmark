Once upon a time, there was a condition known as Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (also called myalgic encephalomyelitis), which can be categorized as both psychiatric and infectious/viral in nature or sometimes it's idiopathic. The exact cause isn't clear but is believed to involve psychological factors, stress, and viruses similar to those linked with mouse leukemia viruses. Diagnosis often relies on a clinical evaluation since there aren’t specific tests available. Common symptoms include fatigue and physical functioning impairment. Treatments typically involve psychotherapy, physical therapy, and lifestyle modifications. Unfortunately, the prognosis for this condition is usually chronic, leading to quality of life impairments. The pathogenesis isn't fully understood but involves both psychiatric and infectious/viral factors or may be idiopathic in nature. Treating Chronic Fatigue Syndrome can also pose challenges; poor insurance coverage, the possibility that treatment may worsen symptoms, and the need to exclude severe cases from standard treatments are among them.
