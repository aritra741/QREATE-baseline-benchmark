Once upon a time, there was a person living with Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (also known as myalgic encephalomyelitis). The disease was classified in multiple categories—psychiatric, infectious viral, and idiopathic. Researchers believed that psychological factors, stress, and viruses similar to mouse leukemia viruses could be involved in its cause.

To diagnose the condition, doctors relied on clinical evaluation rather than specific tests. Common symptoms included fatigue and physical functioning impairment. Treatments often involved psychotherapy, physical therapy, and lifestyle modifications. While it was recognized as a chronic condition with significant quality of life impairments, treatment faced several challenges such as poor insurance coverage, potential worsening of symptoms during treatment, and the need to rule out severe cases first.
