Once upon a time, there was a genetic and metabolic disease called Acute Intermittent Porphyria (AIP). This condition arises from a mutation in the HMBS gene, which leads to a deficiency of porphobilinogen deaminase. Certain triggers like specific medications, alcohol, fasting, stress, infections, or hormonal changes can set it off.

To diagnose AIP, doctors often conduct laboratory tests, genetic testing, and clinical evaluations. Common symptoms include severe abdominal pain, vomiting, hypertension, tachycardia (racing heartbeat), nausea, constipation, diarrhea, bloating, cardiac arrhythmias, urinary retention, numbness, tingling, burning sensations, motor impairment, respiratory failure, seizures, irritability, depression, anxiety, insomnia, hallucinations, paranoia, disorientation, and altered consciousness. Some people also experience weakness.

Treatment options vary widely and may include injections, oral medications, dietary interventions, lifestyle modifications, or even surgery. Prognosis for AIP can be quite variable; some people recover fully, while others face a high risk of recurrence and might remain asymptomatic at times. One significant challenge in managing this condition is the high risk of recurrence, the need to avoid known triggers, and the importance of timely treatment.
