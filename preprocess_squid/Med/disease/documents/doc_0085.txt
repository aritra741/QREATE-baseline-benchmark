Once upon a time, there was a genetic and metabolic disease called Acute Intermittent Porphyria. It happens because of a mutation in the HMBS gene, which affects an enzyme called porphobilinogen deaminase. Certain factors like specific drugs, alcohol, fasting, stress, infections, and hormonal changes can trigger this condition.

To diagnose it, doctors rely on laboratory tests, genetic testing, and clinical evaluations. Common symptoms include abdominal pain, vomiting, hypertension, tachycardia, nausea, constipation, diarrhea, abdominal distension, cardiac arrhythmias, urinary retention, numbness, tingling, burning sensations, motor impairment, respiratory failure, seizures, irritability, depression, anxiety, insomnia, hallucinations, paranoia, disorientation, and altered consciousness. Treatment options include injections, oral medications, dietary interventions, lifestyle modifications, and in some cases, surgery.

The prognosis is variable; patients can experience full recovery, but there's a high risk of recurrence. It's crucial to avoid triggers and seek treatment promptly to manage the condition effectively.
