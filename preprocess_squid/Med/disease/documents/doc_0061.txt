Once upon a time, there was a disease called Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), which is both autoimmune and inflammatory in nature. The doctors don't have all the information about its cause yet, but they do use clinical evaluation to diagnose it. Common symptoms include a rash, fever, mouth ulcers, and weight loss.

To manage SLE, treatments can involve oral medications, injections, surgery, lifestyle modifications, dietary interventions, or physical therapy. Its prognosis is often described as chronic, with potential for disability and quality of life impairment, though outcomes can vary greatly from person to person. The autoimmune and inflammatory pathogenesis make treatment challenging due to high costs, a shortage of rheumatologists, and limited access to care.
