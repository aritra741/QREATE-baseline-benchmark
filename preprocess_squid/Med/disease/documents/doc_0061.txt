Once upon a time, there was a disease called Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) that was both autoimmune and inflammatory. Although the exact cause of SLE wasn't fully understood (due to missing information), doctors often diagnosed it through clinical evaluations. Common symptoms included a rash, fever, mouth ulcers, and weight loss. Treatments could involve oral medications, injections, surgery, lifestyle modifications, dietary interventions, and physical therapy. The prognosis for those with SLE was generally considered chronic, potentially leading to disabilities or quality of life impairments, though outcomes varied widely. One challenge in managing the condition involved high costs, a scarcity of rheumatologists, and limited access to proper care.
