Once upon a time, there was a mysterious condition known as Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH). This disease has no known cause, making it quite enigmatic. Doctors use various diagnostic methods like clinical evaluations, imaging tests, and laboratory assessments to identify IIH. Common symptoms include blurred vision, swelling in the optic nerve (papilloedema), fluid buildup around the center of the retina (macular oedema), and abnormalities in the visual field.

The treatment for this condition often involves oral medication, but there can be challenges as some patients may not respond well to these standard medical therapies. Despite the uncertainties, many people experience partial recovery, although the outcome varies from person to person.
