Once upon a time, there was a condition known as Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH), which falls under the category of idiopathic diseases. The exact cause, or etiology, isn't fully understood, but doctors diagnose it through clinical evaluations, imaging, laboratory tests, and functional assessments.

People with IIH often experience symptoms such as blurred vision, papilloedema (swelling of the optic disc), macular oedema (fluid accumulation in the retina), and visual field abnormalities. The treatment usually involves oral medication, though sometimes there can be a poor response to these standard medical therapies.

In terms of recovery, outcomes vary; some people may experience only partial recovery.
