Once upon a time, there was a mysterious condition known as Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH), which fell under the category of idiopathic diseases. There wasn't much known about its cause (due to the 'nan' value for etiology). Doctors would diagnose IIH through various methods including clinical evaluation, imaging, laboratory tests, and functional tests. The common symptoms included blurred vision, papilloedema, macular oedema, and visual field abnormalities. Treatment often involved oral medication, but the prognosis was a bit uncertain with only partial recovery in some cases and variable outcomes overall. Interestingly, the exact pathogenesis remained idiopathic. One of the main challenges in treating IIH was that it could have a poor response to standard medical therapies.
