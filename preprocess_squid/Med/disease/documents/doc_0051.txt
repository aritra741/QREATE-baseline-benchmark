Once upon a time, there was a mysterious condition known as Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension, or IIH for short. This disease doesn't have a clear cause, which makes it a bit of an enigma ($idiopathic$). To diagnose it, doctors rely on a combination of clinical evaluation, imaging, laboratory tests, and functional assessments.

People with IIH often experience symptoms like blurred vision, papilloedema (swelling of the optic disc), macular oedema (fluid buildup in the eye), and visual field abnormalities. The good news is that treatments usually involve oral medications, which can help manage the condition. However, some individuals may only achieve partial recovery, with outcomes varying widely.

The exact pathogenesis remains somewhat of a mystery since it's idiopathic, much like the disease itself. One challenge in treating IIH is that some patients don't respond well to standard medical therapies, making it tricky to find the right treatment plan for each individual.
