So, let's talk about Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension. It's a condition where the pressure inside the skull increases for unknown reasons ($idiopathic$). While we don't know exactly what causes it ($nan$), doctors use a combination of clinical evaluation, imaging, laboratory tests, and functional tests to diagnose it.

The most common symptoms include blurred vision, swelling in the optic disc (papilloedema), fluid buildup in the center of the eye (macular oedema), and changes in your peripheral or central vision (visual field abnormality).

Treatment usually involves oral medication. However, there can be challenges as some people don't respond well to standard medical therapy ($poor response to standard medical therapy$). Despite these challenges, most patients have a partial recovery with variable outcomes ($partial_recovery||variable$).
