Once upon a time, there was an autoimmune and inflammatory condition called Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA), which was caused by the overactivity of the immune system that often accompanies psoriasis. To diagnose it, doctors would rely on clinical evaluations, laboratory tests, imaging, and screenings. People with PsA commonly experienced joint stiffness, joint pain, joint swelling, and fatigue.

Treatment for this condition involved various methods such as oral medications, injections, lifestyle modifications, and dietary interventions. While these treatments could help manage the symptoms, the prognosis of PsA was often described as chronic, potentially leading to a decline in quality of life that varied from person to person. The pathogenesis of PsA is rooted in autoimmune and inflammatory processes.

One of the challenges faced by those undergoing treatment for PsA included dealing with side effects and the cardiovascular risks associated with certain medications used to manage the condition.
