Once upon a time, there was a condition called Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA), which is an autoimmune and inflammatory disease. The immune system overactivity in people with PsA often leads to psoriasis as well. Doctors use clinical evaluations, laboratory tests, imaging, and screenings to diagnose it. Common symptoms include joint stiffness, pain, swelling, and fatigue. Treatment can be a mix of oral medications, injections, lifestyle modifications, and dietary interventions. While living with PsA is typically a chronic condition that affects the quality of life in various ways, the severity can vary from person to person. The pathogenesis or development of the disease involves both autoimmune and inflammatory processes. However, one significant challenge in managing PsA is dealing with side effects, especially for some drugs which come with cardiovascular risks.
