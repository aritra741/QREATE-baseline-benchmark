Once upon a time, there was a disease called Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA), which falls under the categories of autoimmune and inflammatory diseases. Its root cause lies in an overactive immune system that can sometimes also be related to psoriasis. To diagnose it, doctors rely on clinical evaluations, laboratory tests, imaging techniques, and even screenings.

Typically, people with PsA experience joint stiffness, joint pain, joint swelling, and fatigue as common symptoms. Treatments for this condition include oral medications, injections, lifestyle modifications, and dietary interventions. While the prognosis of PsA is often chronic and can impact one's quality of life in variable ways, it's essential to understand that there are challenges with treatments, such as side effects and cardiovascular risks associated with some drugs.
