Once upon a time, there was a person who was diagnosed with Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA), an autoimmune and inflammatory condition. The disease started when their immune system became overly active and began to attack healthy cells, leading to psoriasis as well. To diagnose it, doctors relied on clinical evaluations, laboratory tests, imaging, and some screening methods. Common symptoms included joint stiffness, pain, swelling, and general fatigue.

When it came to treatment, various options were available: oral medications, injections, lifestyle modifications, and dietary interventions. The prognosis for PsA is often described as a chronic condition with potential impacts on the quality of life, though outcomes can vary greatly from person to person. Pathogenesis involves autoimmune and inflammatory processes, which can complicate treatment. One challenge in managing this disease is dealing with side effects and the cardiovascular risks associated with certain drugs.
