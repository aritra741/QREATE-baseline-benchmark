Once upon a time, there was a genetic condition called Cystic Fibrosis. This disease happens because of genetic mutations, which can pass from parents to children. The exact ways to diagnose it and the common symptoms that people might experience are still not fully understood, as well as the best treatments available and what to expect in terms of long-term outcomes. However, scientists know that these mutations affect how the body processes salt and water in the lungs and other organs. Despite this knowledge, there are still challenges in effectively treating Cystic Fibrosis.
