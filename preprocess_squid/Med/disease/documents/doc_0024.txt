Once upon a time, someone was diagnosed with Prostate Cancer, which falls under the category of neoplastic diseases. The diagnostic methods used included laboratory tests, screening, clinical evaluation, and imaging. Common symptoms they experienced were urinary obstruction and an enlarged prostate. Treatment options often involved surgery, hormone therapy, or chemotherapy. Unfortunately, the prognosis wasn't very encouraging, with high mortality rates and potential progressive quality of life impairment. The pathogenesis behind it is neoplastic changes in cells. One challenge with treatment was managing side effects and the risk of renal failure.
