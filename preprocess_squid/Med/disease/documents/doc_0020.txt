Once upon a time, there was a child diagnosed with Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), which falls under the psychiatric category. The doctors primarily rely on clinical evaluations for diagnosis, and common symptoms include behavioral issues, developmental delays, learning disabilities, mood disorders, and irritability. Treatments often involve a combination of oral medications, psychotherapy, and lifestyle modifications. Unfortunately, ADHD tends to be a chronic condition that can significantly impact quality of life, and its severity varies from person to person. Overdiagnosis, overtreatment, polypharmacy, side effects, drug abuse, diversion, and poor long-term efficacy are some of the challenges faced in managing this disorder.
