Once upon a time, there was a child diagnosed with Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), which falls under the psychiatric category. The main methods for diagnosing ADHD involve clinical evaluation, where doctors look out for common symptoms like behavioral issues, developmental delays, learning disabilities, mood disorders, and irritability. Treatment options include oral medications, psychotherapy, and lifestyle modifications. Despite these interventions, living with ADHD can be a chronic condition that impacts the quality of life, with variability in how it affects different individuals over time. Challenges in treatment include concerns about overdiagnosis, overtreatment, polypharmacy, side effects, drug abuse, diversion, and poor long-term efficacy.
