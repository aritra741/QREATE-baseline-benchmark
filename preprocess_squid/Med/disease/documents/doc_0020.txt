Once upon a time, there was a child named Alex who was diagnosed with Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), which falls under the psychiatric category. While the exact cause of ADHD wasn't clear ($nan$), doctors relied on clinical evaluations to diagnose it. Common symptoms included behavioral issues, developmental delays, and learning disabilities, along with mood disorders, irritability, and more.

To help manage these challenges, a combination of treatments was often used: oral medication, psychotherapy, and lifestyle modifications. Despite these efforts, living with ADHD could be quite difficult, as it was considered a chronic condition that significantly impacted the quality of life. The prognosis varied widely among individuals. Some common treatment challenges included overdiagnosis, overtreatment, polypharmacy (taking multiple medications), side effects, drug abuse, diversion (misuse of drugs), and poor long-term efficacy.
