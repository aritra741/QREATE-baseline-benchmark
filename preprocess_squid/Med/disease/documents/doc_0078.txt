Once upon a time, there was a person living with Alzheimer's Disease, a degenerative condition. The disease could be influenced by genetic, physical, or environmental factors. Doctors would often diagnose it through clinical evaluation. Common symptoms included short-term memory impairment, word-finding problems, direction finding difficulties, repetitive questions, and difficulty managing familiar tasks. Treatments usually involved oral medication, but the condition was chronic and progressive, impacting the quality of life. The degenerative process meant that there was no known cure yet, presenting significant challenges in treatment.
