Once upon a time, there was a group of researchers studying an unusual condition called "Birth Control Heart Attack." This disease falls under the categories of vascular and iatrogenic issues. It often arises from the use of birth control pills containing drospirenone and ethinyl estradiol. To diagnose this condition, doctors rely on clinical evaluations, laboratory tests, and imaging techniques.

Patients with this condition frequently experience symptoms such as chest pain, chest pressure, or chest squeezing, alongside arm pain, jaw pain, neck pain, stomach pain, and fatigue. Treatment options include surgery, drug therapy, lifestyle modifications, and other approaches. The prognosis is often concerning, with high mortality rates and potential quality of life impairments, though outcomes can vary from case to case due to the vascular nature of the disease.
