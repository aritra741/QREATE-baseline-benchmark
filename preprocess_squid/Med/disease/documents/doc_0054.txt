Once upon a time, in a world grappling with HIV, there was an interesting condition called Immune Reconstitution Syndrome (IRS). This syndrome occurred when patients started HAART ( Highly Active Antiretroviral Therapy) and their immune system began to recover. Doctors identified IRS through clinical evaluations and lab tests, noting various symptoms like fever, shortness of breath, stomach pain, diarrhea, headaches, weakness, swollen lymph nodes, lumps under the skin, and unusual rashes.

The treatments for IRS varied widely, including oral medications and injections. The prognosis was also quite variable; while many patients recovered fully, others faced significant health issues or, in some rare cases, even died. The pathogenesis behind IRS involved a mix of inflammatory responses, bacterial infections, viral infections, fungal infections, and sometimes iatrogenic factors due to the therapy itself.

And so, managing IRS became both a diagnostic and therapeutic challenge, requiring careful monitoring and personalized care for each patient.
