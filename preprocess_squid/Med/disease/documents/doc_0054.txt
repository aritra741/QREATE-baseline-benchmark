In the case of a patient diagnosed with Immune Reconstitution Syndrome (IRS), this condition can occur when HIV-infected patients start HAART ( Highly Active Antiretroviral Therapy). The diagnostic process involves both clinical evaluation and laboratory tests to identify common symptoms such as fever, dyspnea, abdominal pain, diarrhea, headache, weakness, lymphadenitis, subcutaneous nodules, and abnormal skin lesions. Treatments often include oral medications or injections, and the prognosis can be variable, with significant morbidity in some cases and occasional mortality. The pathogenesis of IRS can involve inflammatory responses, bacterial infections, viral infections, fungal infections, or iatrogenic factors.
