Once upon a time, a person was diagnosed with Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD), which includes Atherosclerotic Peripheral Arterial Disease and Arm Artery Disease. The disease type is vascular and degenerative, often caused by conditions like atherosclerosis, smoking, diabetes, high blood cholesterol, and high blood pressure.

To diagnose the condition, doctors would conduct clinical evaluations, screenings, laboratory tests, imaging, or functional tests. Common symptoms include intermittent claudication (pain when walking), pain when climbing stairs, and general pain, numbness, aching, and heaviness in muscles. The legs might experience cramping in various areas like the calves, feet, and thighs, accompanied by weak or absent pulses, sores on toes, feet, and legs with slow wound healing.

The skin might also show color changes such as paleness or cyanosis, and the temperature of one leg could be lower than the other. You might notice decreased nail growth, hair loss on toes, and reduced hair growth on legs. Erectile dysfunction is another potential symptom for men.

Treatment options include lifestyle modifications like diet and exercise, oral medications, surgeries if necessary, and sometimes other interventions. The disease is a chronic condition that can lead to disability and significantly impact the quality of life, with the risk of progressive worsening despite treatment. Poor adherence to treatments remains a significant challenge in managing this condition.
