Once upon a time, someone was diagnosed with Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD), which includes Atherosclerotic PAD and Arm Artery Disease. This condition affects the blood flow in their legs, leading to Circulation Problems and Leg Artery Disease. The disease is typically caused by Atherosclerosis, along with factors like smoking, diabetes, high blood cholesterol, and high blood pressure.

To diagnose this condition, doctors might conduct a clinical evaluation, perform screenings, run laboratory tests, use imaging techniques, or carry out functional tests. Common symptoms include Intermittent Claudication (pain when walking), pain when climbing stairs, general pain, numbness, aching, heaviness in muscles, cramping in legs, cramping in thighs, cramping in calves, and cramping in feet. Other signs might be a weak or absent pulse in the legs, sores on toes, feet, or legs that heal slowly, changes in skin color like paleness or cyanosis (a bluish discoloration), decreased temperature in one leg, poor nail growth, and reduced hair growth on toes and legs.

Treatment options vary from Lifestyle Modifications to oral medications and even Surgery. However, treatment can be challenging due to Poor Adherence by the patient and the condition's Progressive nature despite treatment. The prognosis is often viewed as a Chronic Condition that may impair Quality of Life and potentially lead to Disability.
