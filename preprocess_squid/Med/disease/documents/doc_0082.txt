Once upon a time, there was a condition known as Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD), which can also be referred to as Atherosclerotic PAD or Arm Artery Disease. This disease affects the arteries and leads to circulation problems, often manifesting in Leg Artery Disease. The etiology of this condition is often linked to atherosclerosis but can also be influenced by other factors such as smoking, diabetes, high blood cholesterol levels, and high blood pressure.

To diagnose PAD, healthcare providers might conduct a clinical evaluation or screening tests, followed by laboratory tests, imaging studies, or functional tests. Common symptoms include intermittent claudication (pain when walking), pain when climbing stairs, general pain, numbness, aching, heaviness in muscles, cramping in legs, cramping in thighs, cramping in calves, and even cramping in feet.

The disease can affect the pulse in your legs, leading to weak or absent pulses, which might result in sores on toes, feet, or legs that heal slowly. You might also notice changes in skin color like paleness or cyanosis (a blue tinge), a decrease in leg temperature, and even poor nail growth or decreased hair growth on the toes and legs.

Treatment options for PAD include lifestyle modifications to help manage symptoms, oral medications to address underlying conditions, surgery when necessary, and other treatments as needed. The prognosis of PAD is often considered a chronic condition that can lead to disability if not managed properly, with potential impacts on quality of life and progressive worsening despite treatment due to poor adherence or the progression of the disease itself.
