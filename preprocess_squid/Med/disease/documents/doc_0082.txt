Once upon a time, there was a disease called Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD), also known as Atherosclerotic PAD or Arm Artery Disease. This condition affects the blood flow to the legs and arms and can lead to Circulation Problems and Leg Artery Disease. The disease is mainly due to Atherosclerosis, but other factors like smoking, diabetes, high blood cholesterol, and high blood pressure also play a role.

To diagnose this condition, doctors use clinical evaluation, screening, laboratory tests, imaging, and functional tests. Common symptoms include Intermittent Claudication (pain when walking), Pain when climbing stairs, regular pain, numbness, aching, heaviness in muscles, cramping in legs, cramping in thighs, cramping in calves, cramping in feet, weak pulse in legs, absent pulse in legs, sores on toes, sores on feet, and sores on legs with slow wound healing or poor wound healing. The skin might also show color changes like paleness or cyanosis, and the temperature of one leg could be lower than the other.

Treatment options for PAD include Lifestyle Modification (like quitting smoking and managing diabetes), Oral Medication to control blood pressure and cholesterol, Surgery if necessary, and sometimes even other treatments depending on the patient's condition. The prognosis can vary; it might become a Chronic Condition that impacts Quality of Life or leads to Disability with progressive symptoms despite treatment challenges like Poor Adherence from patients.

The pathogenesis is primarily Vascular Degenerative changes due to Atherosclerosis, and one of the main treatment challenges is poor adherence by patients, which can lead to the disease progressing even when treated.
