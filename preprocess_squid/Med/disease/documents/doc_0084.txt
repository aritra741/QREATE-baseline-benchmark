Once upon a time, there was a condition called Preeclampsia. It's a tricky mix of vascular and inflammatory issues, sometimes linked to autoimmune disorders or genetic factors, and can also arise without any clear cause. The main signs include high blood pressure, protein in the urine (proteinuria), swelling in the feet and hands, edema, headaches, abdominal pain, and even reduced urination. Doctors diagnose it through a clinical evaluation and laboratory tests. Managing Preeclampsia usually involves a combination of oral medication, injections, lifestyle changes, and dietary interventions. The good news is that while its outcomes can vary significantly, leading in some cases to high mortality, we still don't have a definitive treatment for it until the baby is delivered.
