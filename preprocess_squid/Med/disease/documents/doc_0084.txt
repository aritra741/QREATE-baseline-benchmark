Once upon a time, there was a disease called Preeclampsia. It's known to have vascular, inflammatory, or idiopathic types. The main causes can be due to blood vessel issues, genetic factors, autoimmune disorders, or insufficient blood flow into the uterus. Doctors diagnose it through clinical evaluations and laboratory tests. Common symptoms include high blood pressure, proteinuria (protein in urine), swelling of feet and hands, edema, headaches, abdominal pain, and reduced urination. Treatments often involve oral medications, injections, lifestyle modifications, and dietary interventions. The prognosis is variable and can be quite serious, with a high mortality rate. Preeclampsia's pathogenesis involves vascular, inflammatory, or idiopathic factors. One of the key challenges in treating it is that there isn't yet a definitive cure, and treatment often requires waiting until delivery to fully address the condition.
