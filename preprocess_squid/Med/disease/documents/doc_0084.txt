Once upon a time, there was a condition called Preeclampsia, which could manifest in three main categories: vascular issues, genetic factors, and autoimmune disorders. Sometimes, it also resulted from insufficient blood flow into the uterus. Doctors would often diagnose it through clinical evaluation or laboratory tests. Common symptoms included high blood pressure, proteinuria (protein in urine), swelling of feet, swelling of hands, edema (swelling due to fluid retention), headaches, abdominal pain, and reduced urination. Treatment could involve oral medication, injections, lifestyle modifications, or dietary interventions. The prognosis was quite variable and unfortunately, had a relatively high mortality rate. Preeclampsia's pathogenesis mirrored its diagnostic complexity, involving vascular, inflammatory, and idiopathic factors. Treating it was challenging as there was no definitive treatment available until delivery.
