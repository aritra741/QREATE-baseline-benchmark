Once upon a time, there was a condition called Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD), which falls under the category of an inflammatory disease. The root cause often lies in abnormalities of the antireflux barrier, specifically the lower esophageal sphincter.

To diagnose GERD, doctors might use clinical evaluation, endoscopy, or functional tests. Common symptoms include heartburn, chest pain, chronic hoarseness, persistent cough, and regurgitation. Treatment options range from oral medications to lifestyle modifications, and in some cases, surgery might be necessary.

The condition is typically considered a chronic one with a high risk of recurrence, which can significantly impact the quality of life. The pathogenesis involves inflammation, which complicates treatment because patients may face challenges like drug resistance, frequent relapses, side effects from medications, or poor adherence to treatment regimens.
