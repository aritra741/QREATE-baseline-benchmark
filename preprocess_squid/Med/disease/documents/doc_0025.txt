Once upon a time, there was a person who was diagnosed with Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD), which is an inflammatory condition. The cause of their GERD was due to abnormalities in the lower esophageal sphincter, acting as the antireflux barrier. To diagnose the issue, doctors conducted both clinical evaluations and endoscopies, along with some functional tests.

Common symptoms experienced by this person included heartburn, chest pain, chronic hoarseness, chronic cough, and regurgitation. The treatment for GERD usually involved a combination of oral medications, lifestyle modifications, and in some cases, surgery.

Unfortunately, living with GERD often meant dealing with it as a chronic condition, which came with a high risk of recurrence and significantly impacted their quality of life. Doctors found that challenges like drug resistance, frequent relapses, side effects from the medication, and poor adherence to treatment plans made managing GERD quite difficult.
