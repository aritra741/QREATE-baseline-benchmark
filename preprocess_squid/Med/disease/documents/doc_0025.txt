Once upon a time, there was a person who suffered from Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD), an inflammatory condition. The root of the problem lay in abnormalities of the antireflux barrier, specifically the lower esophageal sphincter. To diagnose the issue, doctors conducted clinical evaluations, performed endoscopies, and ran functional tests.

The person often experienced heartburn, chest pain, chronic hoarseness, a persistent cough, and regurgitation. The treatment journey involved various methods including oral medications, lifestyle modifications, and even surgery in some cases. Despite these efforts, the condition was often chronic with a high risk of recurrence and significant quality-of-life impairment.

The pathogenesis behind GERD was also marked by inflammation, making it challenging to manage. Patients faced several hurdles such as drug resistance, frequent relapses, side effects from medications, and poor adherence to treatment plans.
