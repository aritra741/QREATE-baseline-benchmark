Once upon a time, a person was diagnosed with Bell's palsy, an idiopathic, inflammatory, or infectious viral condition. The disease often stems from the reactivation of latent herpes simplex virus or varicella zoster virus, or it could be due to an unknown cause. To diagnose it, doctors might use imaging, clinical evaluation, laboratory tests, or functional tests. Common symptoms include acute peripheral facial paralysis or facial weakness. Treatments usually involve oral medication or injection. The prognosis can vary—some people make a full recovery, while others may have partial recovery. The pathogenesis of Bell's palsy is often linked to infectious viral causes, inflammation, or idiopathic reasons. Unfortunately, there isn't a specific treatment for nonrecovering cases.
