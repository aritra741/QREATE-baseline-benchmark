Once upon a time, there was a condition called Bell's palsy. It could be idiopathic (unknown cause), inflammatory, or due to the reactivation of either latent herpes simplex virus or varicella zoster virus. To diagnose it, doctors might use imaging, clinical evaluation, laboratory tests, or functional tests. Common symptoms included acute peripheral facial paralysis and facial weakness. Treatments often involved oral medication or injections. The prognosis could vary; some people fully recovered, others had partial recovery, while the outcome was variable for many. Pathogenesis could be due to infectious viral factors, inflammation, or an idiopathic cause. One challenge in treatment was that no established treatment existed for nonrecovering cases.
