Once upon a time, there was a condition known as Bell's palsy. It can be idiopathic (unknown cause), inflammatory, or infectious viral in nature, often due to the reactivation of latent herpes simplex virus or varicella zoster virus. Doctors might use imaging, clinical evaluation, laboratory tests, or functional tests for diagnosis. The most common symptoms include acute peripheral facial paralysis and facial weakness. Treatment can involve oral medication or injection, with outcomes ranging from full recovery to partial recovery, sometimes variable. Pathogenesis is often linked to infectious viral causes but can also be inflammatory or idiopathic. One challenge in treating Bell's palsy is that there isn't a specific treatment for cases that do not recover fully.
