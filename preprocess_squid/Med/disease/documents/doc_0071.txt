Once upon a time, there was a disease called Celiac Disease. It's known to be both genetic and autoimmune in nature. The disease often starts when someone with a genetic predisposition consumes gluten. To diagnose it, doctors rely on clinical evaluations. Common symptoms include headache, stomachache, fatigue, swelling in the gut, trouble digesting food, diarrhea, bloating, indigestion, and sometimes difficulties breathing or moving arms or legs.

For treatment, dietary intervention is key; people with Celiac Disease must avoid gluten entirely. Sometimes, oral medication can help as well. While we have effective treatments, there are no known challenges specifically linked to Celiac Disease's management at this time. The exact pathogenesis involves genetic mutations and an autoimmune response.
