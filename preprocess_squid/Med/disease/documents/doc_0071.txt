Once upon a time, there was a disease called Celiac Disease. This disease has two main types—both genetic and autoimmune. When someone with a genetic predisposition eats gluten, the immune system reacts by attacking the small intestine, leading to various symptoms.

Common signs of this condition include headaches, stomachaches, fatigue, swelling in the gut, difficulty digesting food, diarrhea, bloating, indigestion, trouble breathing, issues swallowing, and even problems moving arms or legs. To diagnose Celiac Disease, doctors rely on clinical evaluations.

The good news is that there are treatment options available, mainly dietary changes and oral medications to help manage symptoms effectively. However, the exact long-term outcomes for people with this disease aren't fully understood yet, as the prognosis data isn't available. The pathogenesis of Celiac Disease involves genetic mutations leading to an autoimmune response against gluten.

Despite these challenges, managing the condition through a strict diet can significantly improve quality of life.
