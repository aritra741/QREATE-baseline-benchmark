Once there was a person dealing with Major Depressive Disorder, a psychiatric condition. The causes for their illness included injury, chronic pain, financial hardship, family strife, and the belief that returning to employment seemed improbable. To diagnose the issue, healthcare professionals relied on clinical evaluations. Common symptoms reported were depressed mood, irritability, forgetfulness, impatience, lethargy, neglecting self-care, and frequent complaining. Treatment options included psychotherapy and oral medications. However, there was a high risk of recurrence. Challenges in treatment often involved poor adherence to the prescribed regimen, discontinuation of medication, or patients adjusting their dosage on their own.
