Once upon a time, there was a person diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder, which falls under the category of psychiatric diseases. The doctor found that the disorder could be linked to various factors like injury, chronic pain, financial hardship, family strife, and a belief that returning to employment seemed improbable. To diagnose it, they relied on clinical evaluations. Common symptoms included depressed mood, irritability, forgetfulness, impatience, lethargy, neglecting self-care, and complaining bitterly. Treatment options typically involved psychotherapy and oral medications. However, there were challenges such as poor adherence, discontinuing medication, and self-adjustment of dosages. Despite these difficulties, the prognosis had a high risk of recurrence.
