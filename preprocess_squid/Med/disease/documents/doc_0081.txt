Once upon a time, someone was diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder, which falls under the psychiatric category. The doctors found that their condition might be linked to injury, chronic pain, financial hardship, family strife, or believing it's improbable to return to employment. To diagnose them, clinicians conducted thorough clinical evaluations. Common symptoms included feeling depressed, being irritable and forgetful, dealing with impatience, showing lethargy, neglecting self-care, and complaining bitterly. Treatment involved a combination of psychotherapy and oral medications. However, the prognosis indicated a high risk of recurrence. Challenges in treatment often revolved around poor adherence to therapy, discontinuing medication on their own, or adjusting dosages without medical supervision.
