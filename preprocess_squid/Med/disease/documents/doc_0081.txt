Once upon a time, someone was diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder, which falls under the psychiatric category. The doctors identified that their condition might have been triggered by various factors such as injury, chronic pain, financial hardship, family strife, and the belief that returning to employment seemed improbable. To diagnose this, they went through a clinical evaluation process.

The person often experienced common symptoms like depressed mood, irritability, forgetfulness, impatience, lethargy, neglecting self-care, and constantly complaining bitterly. The treatments usually included psychotherapy sessions and oral medication prescribed by the doctor. Despite these efforts, there was a high risk of recurrence.

However, treatment challenges could arise due to poor adherence, discontinuation of medication, or even self-adjustment of dosage without consulting a healthcare professional.
