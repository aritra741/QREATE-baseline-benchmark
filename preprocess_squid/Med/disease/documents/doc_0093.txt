Once upon a time, there was a patient diagnosed with Drug Resistant Tuberculosis (DR TB), an infectious disease. The cause of DR TB involves drug-resistant bacteria and typical bacterial infections. To diagnose it, doctors rely on laboratory tests and clinical evaluations. Common symptoms include persistent coughing and general weakness.

The treatment for DR TB usually starts with oral medications, but there are challenges. Drug resistance can make the response to first-line drugs poor. Despite these difficulties, some patients recover fully, while others face a high risk of mortality due to the disease's pathogenic nature as an infectious bacterial condition.
