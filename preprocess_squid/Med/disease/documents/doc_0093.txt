Once upon a time, there was a mysterious disease called Drug Resistant Tuberculosis (DR TB), which falls under the category of infectious diseases. The root causes include drug resistance and bacterial infection. To diagnose it, doctors rely on laboratory tests and clinical evaluation. Common symptoms include persistent coughing and general weakness.

When treating DR TB, oral medications are often used, but there are challenges to this approach due to drug resistance and inadequate responses to first-line drugs. Despite these hurdles, the prognosis can be positive for some patients, leading to full recovery, while others face a high risk of mortality. This condition is driven by its infectious nature and bacterial origins, making it a complex health issue.
