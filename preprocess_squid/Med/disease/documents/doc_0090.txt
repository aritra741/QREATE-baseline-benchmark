Once upon a time, there was a disease called Peritonitis. It was either infectious or inflammatory in nature and often caused by germs like Staphylococcus aureus or Ewingella americana. The disruption of the peritoneum, catheterization, or even hospital water sources could also lead to this condition. Doctors would usually diagnose it through clinical evaluation and laboratory tests.

People with Peritonitis might experience generalized abdominal pain, a loss of appetite, left chest pain, pressure, tenderness, and a positive rebound sign when pressed. The treatment often involved injections, but the prognosis wasn't always clear. The disease's pathogenesis was rooted in infectious bacterial causes or inflammatory responses.
