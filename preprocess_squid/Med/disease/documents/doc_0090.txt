Once upon a time, there was a disease called Peritonitis. It could be either infectious or inflammatory in nature. The common culprits behind this condition are bacteria like Staphylococcus aureus and Ewingella americana, as well as disruptions to the peritoneum from catheterization or hospital water sources. Doctors usually diagnose it through clinical evaluation and laboratory tests.

Peritonitis often causes symptoms such as generalized abdominal pain, a decreased appetite, left chest pain, pressure, tenderness, and a positive rebound sign. The treatment typically involves injections. Though the pathogenesis is primarily infectious bacterial or inflammatory, there are no specific challenges mentioned in treating this condition.
