Once upon a time, there was a disease known as Charcot Neuropathic Osteoarthropathy. This condition could be categorized under metabolic, degenerative, inflammatory, traumatic, vascular, or idiopathic types. It often arose due to diabetes mellitus, sensory-motor neuropathy, autonomic neuropathy, trauma, metabolic bone abnormalities, alcoholism, rheumatoid arthritis, and sometimes even for unknown reasons.

To diagnose this condition, doctors relied on clinical evaluation, imaging techniques, biopsy, and laboratory tests. Patients frequently reported swollen feet, warm foot sensation, redness (erythema), pain, increased foot size, swelling (oedema), elevated temperature (hyperthermia), reduced sensation, or a lack of feeling in the affected area (pallanaesthesia).

Treatment options included physical therapy, surgery, lifestyle modifications, and other methods. The prognosis for those affected was mixed; some faced high mortality rates, while others endured disability or progressive issues that impacted their quality of life variably.

Understanding its pathogenesis helped pinpoint factors like metabolic disorders, inflammation, degeneration, trauma, vascular issues, or idiopathic causes. Challenges in treatment often stemmed from poor adherence to therapy, a lack of effective pharmacological interventions, the risk of relapse, comorbidities, and surgical risks.
