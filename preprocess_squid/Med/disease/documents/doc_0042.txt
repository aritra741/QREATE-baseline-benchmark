Once upon a time, someone was dealing with low back pain. The condition could be due to musculoskeletal strain, a herniated disc, compressed nerve root, degenerative disc disease, joint disease, vertebral fracture, tumor, inflammatory condition, infection, gallbladder disease, kidney stone, or even an aortic aneurysm. To diagnose the issue, doctors might rely on clinical evaluation and imaging tests.

The common symptoms included lower back pain, tenderness, diffuse pain, coordination impairment, bladder control loss, bowel control loss, and sleep disturbance. Treatment options could range from oral medication to physical therapy, lifestyle modification, surgery, injection, or even dietary intervention. The prognosis varied; it could lead to full recovery, partial recovery, a chronic condition, disability, or something that changed over time.

Interestingly, treating the disease came with its own set of challenges, including dealing with side effects, the risk of addiction, and negative drug interactions.
