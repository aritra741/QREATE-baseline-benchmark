Once upon a time, there was a patient experiencing low back pain. The medical team identified the condition as either degenerative, traumatic, inflammatory, infectious, neoplastic, idiopathic, or others based on the specific cause, which could be musculoskeletal strain, herniated disc, compressed nerve root, degenerative disc, joint disease, vertebral fracture, tumor, inflammatory condition, infection, gallbladder disease, kidney stone, kidney infection, or aortic aneurysm. To diagnose the issue, they relied on clinical evaluation and imaging techniques.

Common symptoms included lower back pain, tenderness, diffuse pain, coordination impairment, bladder control loss, bowel control loss, and sleep disturbance. The treatment plan might involve oral medication, physical therapy, lifestyle modification, surgery, injection, or dietary intervention. The prognosis could be full recovery, partial recovery, a chronic condition, disability, or variable depending on the case.

Pathogenesis was often due to degenerative processes, inflammation, trauma, infectious bacterial agents, neoplastic conditions, idiopathic reasons, or other factors. Treatment challenges included side effects, addiction risk, and negative drug interactions.
