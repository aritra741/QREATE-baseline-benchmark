Once upon a time, there was a condition called Low Back Pain (LBP), which can be caused by several factors including musculoskeletal strain, herniated disc, compressed nerve root, degenerative disc, joint disease, vertebral fracture, tumor, inflammatory condition, infection, gallbladder disease, kidney stone, kidney infection, and aortic aneurysm. Doctors often use clinical evaluation and imaging to diagnose LBP. Common symptoms include lower back pain, tenderness, diffuse pain, coordination impairment, bladder control loss, bowel control loss, and sleep disturbance. Treatments vary widely and can involve oral medication, physical therapy, lifestyle modification, surgery, injection, or dietary intervention. The prognosis for LBP can range from full recovery to a chronic condition with possible disability, and in some cases, it's variable. Pathogenesis can be degenerative, inflammatory, traumatic, infectious bacterial, neoplastic, idiopathic, or other. One big challenge in treating LBP is dealing with side effects, addiction risk, and negative drug interactions.
