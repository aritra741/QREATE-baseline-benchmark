Once upon a time, there was a disease called Huntington's disease or Huntington's Chorea, which fell under the umbrella of genetic, degenerative, and psychiatric disorders. The root cause of this condition was due to a genetic disorder characterized by abnormal CAG repeats in a specific gene. While there were no established diagnostic methods at that time, those affected experienced a wide range of symptoms including movement disorders, behavioral changes, and psychiatric issues like low eye movements, muscle stress, communication difficulties, loss of confidence, trouble with simple tasks, changes in attitude, loss of interest, decreased sexual desire, learning disabilities, sadness, forced smiles, reduced energy, and fatigue. The treatments included oral medications and lifestyle modifications, but the prognosis was not good; it was incurable and progressive, leading to a significant impairment in quality of life. The disease process was driven by genetic mutations that led to degeneration, making treatment challenging as there was no proper cure available.
