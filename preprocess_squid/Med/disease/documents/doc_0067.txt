Once upon a time, there was a disease called Huntington's Disease or Huntington's Chorea. This condition had three main types: genetic, degenerative, and psychiatric. It was caused by a genetic disorder, specifically due to abnormal CAG repeats in the gene. While there weren't any specific diagnostic methods known at the time, it often showed up with movement disorders, behavioral changes, and psychiatric issues like low eye movements, muscle stress, communication difficulties, loss of confidence, trouble with simple tasks, changes in attitude, loss of interest, decreased sexual desire, learning difficulties, sadness, forced smiles, reduced energy, and fatigue.

There were treatments available that included oral medication and lifestyle modifications, but the disease was unfortunately incurable and progressive. It significantly impaired the quality of life for those affected. The underlying pathogenesis was linked to genetic mutation and degeneration, making it a challenging condition to treat.
