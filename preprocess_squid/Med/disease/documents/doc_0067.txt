Once upon a time, there was a genetic condition known as Huntington's disease or Huntingdon's Chorea. It was categorized under three main types: genetic, degenerative, and psychiatric. The root cause of this disease lies in an abnormal number of CAG repeats within a specific gene. While the exact diagnostic methods for identifying Huntington's disease are not well-established, the common symptoms include movement disorders, behavioral changes, and psychiatric disorders. Other signs might be low eye movements, muscle stress, communication difficulties, loss of confidence, trouble with simple tasks, a forced smile, loss of energy, and sadness. Treatments typically involve oral medication and lifestyle modifications, but despite these efforts, the disease is considered incurable and progressive, leading to significant quality-of-life impairments. The exact process of how genetic mutations lead to this degenerative condition involves complex pathogenesis, and one of the biggest challenges in treating Huntington's disease is its current lack of a proper cure.
