Once upon a time, there was a genetic condition called Huntington's disease or Huntington's Chorea. This disease falls under the categories of genetic, degenerative, and psychiatric disorders due to its complex nature. The root cause lies in an abnormal number of CAG repeats within a specific gene, making it a genetic disorder.

Movement disorders and behavioral changes are among the common symptoms experienced by those with this condition. Other symptoms include low eye movements, muscle stress, communication difficulties, loss of confidence, trouble completing simple tasks, change in attitude, loss of interest, diminished sexual desire, learning difficulties, sadness, forced smiles, reduced energy levels, and fatigue.

While there are treatments available like oral medication and lifestyle modifications, the disease is currently incurable and progressive, leading to a decline in the quality of life. The underlying mechanism involves genetic mutations that lead to degeneration over time, making it challenging to find a proper cure for this condition.
