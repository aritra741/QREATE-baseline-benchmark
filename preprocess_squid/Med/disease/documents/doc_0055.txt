Once upon a time, there was a person who developed heart failure. This condition can be categorized as degenerative or vascular, and sometimes it might involve other factors too. The causes could range from chronic diseases to trauma, infections, the use of drugs, poisoning, electric shock, heat stroke, tumors, valve diseases, or left-to-right shunts.

To diagnose this complex condition, doctors rely on clinical evaluation and imaging techniques. Common symptoms include collapse, coughing, shallow and rapid breathing, swelling, weakness, weight loss, heart murmurs, a rapid heartbeat, throbbing of the jugular veins, and swelling along the surface of the abdomen.

Treatment for heart failure often includes oral medications and, in some cases, surgery. The prognosis can vary widely—it might be chronic or fluctuate between different states. Interestingly, the exact pathogenesis of heart failure can be degenerative, vascular, or other, making it a multifaceted condition to manage.
