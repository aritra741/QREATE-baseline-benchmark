Once upon a time, there was a patient diagnosed with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC), also known as non-small cell lung cancer or non-squamous NSCLCs and squamous NSCLCs. This disease falls under the category of neoplastic diseases but can also be considered inflammatory in some cases. To diagnose it, doctors rely on clinical evaluation, laboratory tests, and imaging. The common symptoms are not always clearly defined, making diagnosis challenging based solely on symptoms.

The treatment journey for these patients often includes injections, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy. However, the prognosis is quite variable; while some might experience high mortality rates, others may see progressive disease with a significant impact on their quality of life. The pathogenesis involves both neoplastic and inflammatory elements, adding complexity to understanding the disease.

One of the main challenges in treating this condition is that only a minority of patients benefit from immunotherapy due to its high cost and the critical need to identify who will respond positively to these treatments.
