Once upon a time, there was a medicine called Rifaximin. It's used to treat various conditions such as acute gastrointestinal infections, traveler’s diarrhea, small intestinal bacterial overgrowth, hepatic encephalopathy, uncomplicated diverticular disease, chronic intestinal inflammation, and preventing infectious complications in colorectal surgery. The way it works is quite fascinating; it irreversibly binds to the beta subunit of bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, which inhibits RNA and protein synthesis, leading to the killing of bacteria. On the downside, some folks might experience side effects like dizziness, headaches, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, flatulence, loss of appetite, urticarial skin rash, or allergic dermatitis. This powerful drug is made by Alfasigma S.p.A./Alfa Wassermann (Italy).
