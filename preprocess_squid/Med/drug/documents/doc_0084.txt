Once upon a time, there was a drug called Rifaximin. It was used for various conditions like acute gastrointestinal infections, traveler's diarrhea, small intestinal bacterial overgrowth, hepatic encephalopathy, uncomplicated diverticular disease, chronic intestinal inflammation, and prevention of infectious complications in colorectal surgery. Its mechanism of action involved irreversibly binding to the beta subunit of bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, which inhibited RNA and protein synthesis, leading to bactericidal activity. Common side effects included dizziness, headache, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, flatulence, loss of appetite, urticarial skin rash, and allergic dermatitis. The drug was manufactured by Alfasigma S.p.A./Alfa Wassermann (Italy).
