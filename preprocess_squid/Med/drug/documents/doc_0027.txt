Once upon a time, a medication known as 6-mercaptopurine was used to treat several conditions. It had a variety of roles, including treating rheumatoid arthritis, preventing graft rejection, and managing inflammatory bowel disease. The way it worked was quite interesting; it was metabolized by TPMT into 6-methylmercaptopurine, by xanthine oxidase into 6-thiouracil, and by hypoxanthine guanine phosphoribosyl-transferase into 6-thioguanine. While effective, this medication came with some side effects such as myelosuppression, hepatotoxicity, and megaloblastosis.
