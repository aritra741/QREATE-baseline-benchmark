Once upon a time, there was a medication called 6-mercaptopurine. It had many important roles to play in the medical world, treating conditions like rheumatoid arthritis, preventing graft rejection, and managing inflammatory bowel disease. The way it worked was pretty fascinating; after being metabolized by TPMT into 6-methylmercaptopurine, xanthine oxidase turning it into 6-thiouracil, or hypoxanthine guanine phosphoribosyl-transferase converting it to 6-thioguanine, it went about its business. However, like many powerful medicines, it came with a few side effects such as myelosuppression, hepatotoxicity, and megaloblastosis.
