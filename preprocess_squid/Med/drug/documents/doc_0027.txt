Once upon a time, there was a medication called 6-mercaptopurine. This drug had multiple uses, including treating rheumatoid arthritis, preventing graft rejection, and managing inflammatory bowel disease. The way it worked in the body was fascinating—it got broken down into different components like 6-methylmercaptopurine by TPMT, turned into 6-thiouracil with the help of xanthine oxidase, or transformed into 6-thioguanine via hypoxanthine guanine phosphoribosyl-transferase. However, just like any other medicine, it came with its own set of side effects, such as myelosuppression, hepatotoxicity, and megaloblastosis.
