Once upon a time, there was a medication called 6-mercaptopurine. This drug had multiple uses and could help treat rheumatoid arthritis, prevent graft rejection, and manage inflammatory bowel disease. It worked by being metabolized into different compounds depending on the body's enzymes—specifically, it turned into 6-methylmercaptopurine through TPMT, 6-thiouracil via xanthine oxidase, and 6-thioguanine with the help of hypoxanthine guanine phosphoribosyl-transferase. However, just like any other medication, it came with some side effects such as myelosuppression (a decrease in blood cells), hepatotoxicity (liver toxicity), and megaloblastosis (anemia due to vitamin B12 deficiency).
