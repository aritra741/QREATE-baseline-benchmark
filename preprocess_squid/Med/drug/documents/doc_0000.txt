Once upon a time, there was a medication called Pravastatin. It was used to treat high cholesterol and high blood fat levels, as well as to prevent cardiovascular events in people with a history of myocardial infarction or unstable angina. The way it worked was by competitively inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase, which helped reduce cholesterol synthesis, increase the expression of LDL receptors, enhance LDL catabolism, and inhibit VLDL synthesis.

However, just like any other medication, Pravastatin had its side effects. Some patients experienced muscle pain, joint pain, or even muscle cramps and weakness. Elevated levels of creatine kinase were also a concern, along with increased liver enzymes. Other less common issues included dizziness, headaches, sleep disturbances, insomnia, nightmares, vision problems like blurred or double vision, digestive disorders such as abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, bloating, itching, rashes, and hives. Some patients also reported hair changes or loss, difficulty urinating, frequent urination at night (nocturia), peripheral polyneuropathy with paraesthesia, rare cases of anaphylaxis, angioedema, lupus erythematosus-like syndrome, jaundice, hepatitis, and in very rare instances, fulminant hepatic necrosis or rhabdomyolysis. Myopathy could also occur, as well as interstitial lung disease, diabetes mellitus, dermatomyositis, or even more severe conditions like anaphylaxis.
