Once upon a time, there was a medication called Pravastatin. This drug was prescribed for people with high cholesterol and high blood fat levels to help prevent cardiovascular events like heart attacks (myocardial infarction) or unstable angina. It works by competitively inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase—an enzyme involved in cholesterol synthesis—which helps reduce the body's production of cholesterol while also increasing the LDL receptors, thus enhancing the liver’s ability to clear harmful LDL cholesterol from the bloodstream and VLDL (very-low-density lipoprotein) synthesis.

However, just like any other medication, Pravastatin comes with a list of potential side effects. Some users might experience muscle pain or joint discomfort, while others could feel weak or notice muscle cramps. Elevated levels of creatine kinase—a protein that increases in the blood when there's muscle damage—might also be observed. Other common issues can include dizziness and headaches, sleep disturbances like insomnia or nightmares, vision changes such as blurred or double vision, digestive troubles including abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, bloating, itching, rashes, and hives.

In rare cases, more severe side effects could occur, such as hair loss or changes, difficulty urinating, frequent urination at night (nocturia), peripheral polyneuropathy which may cause paraesthesia—a tingling sensation in the skin—or even more serious conditions like anaphylaxis, angioedema, lupus erythematosus-like syndrome, jaundice, hepatitis, fulminant hepatic necrosis, rhabdomyolysis (a breakdown of muscle fibers leading to kidney failure), myopathy (muscle disease), and interstitial lung disease. In very rare instances, Pravastatin might trigger diabetes mellitus or dermatomyositis.

And that’s the story of Pravastatin—its purpose, how it works, and all the things we should be aware of when taking it!
