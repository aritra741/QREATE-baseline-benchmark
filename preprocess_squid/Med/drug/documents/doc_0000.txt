The generic name for this medication is Pravastatin. It's primarily used to treat high cholesterol and high blood fat levels, as well as to prevent cardiovascular events in people with a history of myocardial infarction, unstable angina, or post-transplant hyperlipidemia. Its mechanism of action involves competitively inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase, which reduces cholesterol synthesis, increases LDL receptor expression, enhances the breakdown of LDL cholesterol, and inhibits VLDL (very low-density lipoprotein) production.

Possible side effects include muscle pain, joint pain, muscle cramps, muscle weakness, elevated creatine kinase levels, increased liver enzymes, dizziness, headaches, sleep disturbances such as insomnia or nightmares, vision problems like blurred vision or double vision, digestive issues including abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, bloating, itching, rashes, and hives. Other side effects can include hair changes or loss, difficulty urinating, frequent urination, nocturia (waking up to urinate at night), peripheral neuropathy with sensations like paraesthesia (abnormal skin sensation), as well as more severe reactions such as anaphylaxis, angioedema, lupus erythematosus-like syndrome, jaundice, hepatitis, fulminant hepatic necrosis, rhabdomyolysis, myopathy, interstitial lung disease, diabetes mellitus, and dermatomyositis.
