The generic name for this medication is Pravastatin, which is used to treat high cholesterol and high blood fat levels. It also helps in the prevention of cardiovascular events, especially for those with a history of myocardial infarction, unstable angina, or post-transplant hyperlipidemia. Its mechanism of action involves competitively inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase, which reduces cholesterol synthesis while increasing LDL receptor expression and promoting VLDL catabolism.

Side effects can vary widely among users, including muscle pain, joint pain, muscle cramps, muscle weakness, elevated creatine kinase levels, increased liver enzymes, dizziness, headaches, sleep disturbances, insomnia, nightmares, vision issues like blurred or double vision, digestive disorders such as abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, bloating, itching, rashes, and hives. Some users might experience hair changes or loss, difficulty urinating, frequent urination, or nocturia. Other potential side effects include peripheral polyneuropathy, paraesthesia, anaphylaxis, angioedema, lupus erythematosus-like syndrome, jaundice, hepatitis, fulminant hepatic necrosis, rhabdomyolysis, myopathy, and interstitial lung disease. In rare cases, there have been reports of diabetes mellitus, dermatomyositis as well.
