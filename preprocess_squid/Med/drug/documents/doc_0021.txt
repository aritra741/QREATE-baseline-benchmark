Once upon a time, there was a medication called Metformin. Its primary purpose was to help people with impaired glucose tolerance and prevent the onset of diabetes mellitus. It could also be used in managing post-transplantation diabetes mellitus. Metformin worked by enhancing glucose metabolism, limiting unwanted weight gain, providing cardiovascular protection, improving the metabolic profile, and even showing some anti-neoplastic activity. However, like any other medication, it came with side effects such as gastrointestinal symptoms, indigestion, abdominal pain, and a metallic taste in the mouth.
