Once upon a time, there was a medication called Ursodiol, which has the generic name of Ursodeoxycholic. This versatile drug can be used to prevent gallstones and primary biliary cirrhosis, as well as manage cholestatic conditions. It even offers some promise in chemopreventing colon cancer in cases of inflammatory bowel disease. The mechanism behind its effectiveness is fascinating: it decreases cholesterol production in the liver and absorption in the intestines, helps break down cholesterol gallstones, protects the liver (hepatoprotective), increases bile flow, and induces apoptosis in various types of cancer cells—specifically in those found in the liver, colon, prostate, and breast.
