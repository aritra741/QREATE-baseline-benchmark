Once upon a time, there was a medication called Nebivolol. It was used to treat high blood pressure. Nebivolol works by blocking β-adrenergic receptors, with a preference for the β1 receptor at ≤10 mg doses. This helps decrease heart rate and reduce myocardial contractility, among other effects like diminishing sympathetic outflow, suppressing renin activity, and causing vasodilation.

Now, just like any medication, Nebivolol can come with some side effects. People might experience headaches, feel tired or fatigued, have bradycardia (a slower than normal heart rate), deal with diarrhea, or even feel nauseous. Some might notice chest pain, swelling in their limbs, dizziness, or insomnia. Breathing difficulties and rashes are also possible, along with feelings of weakness or asthenia, abdominal discomfort, increased cholesterol levels, or sensations like tingling (paraesthesia). 

All this was made by a company called Ascend Laboratories, LLC.
