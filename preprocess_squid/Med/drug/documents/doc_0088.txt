Once upon a time, there was a medication called Nebivolol. It was used to treat high blood pressure, and it worked by blocking β-adrenergic receptors in a way that's particularly selective for β1 at doses of 10 mg or less. This helped decrease heart rate, lower the strength of the heartbeat, reduce sympathetic nervous system activity, and even suppress renin release, all while causing vasodilation. Side effects could include headache, fatigue, slow heart rate (bradycardia), diarrhea, nausea, chest pain, swelling in the extremities (peripheral edema), dizziness, trouble sleeping (insomnia), shortness of breath (dyspnea), and skin reactions like a rash or feeling weak (asthenia). All these effects were managed by Ascend Laboratories, LLC, the manufacturer behind this drug.
