Once upon a time, there was a medication called Nebivolol, which was used to treat high blood pressure. This drug worked by blocking β-adrenergic receptors, but it showed preferential selectivity for β1 at ≤10 mg doses. It helped in decreasing heart rate and reducing the strength of heart contractions while also lowering sympathetic nervous system activity, suppressing renin production, causing vasodilation. However, just like any other medication, Nebivolol had a few side effects that some patients might experience, such as headache, fatigue, slow heartbeat (bradycardia), diarrhea, nausea, chest pain, swelling in the legs (peripheral edema), dizziness, trouble sleeping (insomnia), difficulty breathing (dyspnea), rash, feeling weak or tired (asthenia), abdominal pain, high cholesterol levels (hypercholesterolemia), and a tingling sensation (paraesthesia). These side effects were managed by the manufacturer, Ascend Laboratories, LLC.
