Once upon a time, there was a medication called Bupivacaine. It's used for surgical anesthesia and during lengthy restorative procedures to keep patients still. After surgery, it also helps manage postoperative pain. How does it work? Well, Bupivacaine binds to sodium channels in neurons, effectively blocking the nerve impulses from being conducted. However, a potential side effect is that patients might accidentally hurt themselves by biting the anesthetized tissue since they can't feel it properly.
