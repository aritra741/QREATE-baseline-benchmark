Once upon a time, there was a medication called Regorafenib. This drug was used to treat people with metastatic colorectal cancer who had already tried standard therapies and for those with metastatic or unresectable gastrointestinal stromal tumours after they failed treatments with imatinib and sunitinib. Regorafenib works by being taken orally and it targets multiple kinases, which help in stopping tumor growth, preventing new blood vessel formation (angiogenesis), promoting cell death (oncogenesis), and altering the tumor's microenvironment.

However, like all medicines, Regorafenib comes with a list of potential side effects. Patients might experience fatigue, hand-foot-skin reaction, diarrhea, loss of appetite, feeling very tired (asthenia or anorexia), mouth sores (mucositis), weight loss, infection, high blood pressure, changes in voice (dysphonia), liver issues (hepatotoxicity), bleeding, and even rare cases of gastrointestinal perforation. This medication is manufactured by Bayer Inc.
