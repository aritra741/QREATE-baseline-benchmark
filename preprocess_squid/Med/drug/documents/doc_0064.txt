Once upon a time, there was a medication named Regorafenib. It was used for treating metastatic colorectal cancer when other standard therapies had already been tried and didn't work. Regorafenib also helped in managing metastatic or unresectable gastrointestinal stromal tumours after treatments with imatinib and sunitinib. This oral multi-kinase inhibitor worked by affecting various aspects of tumour growth, angiogenesis, oncogenesis, and the tumour microenvironment.

However, just like any other medication, Regorafenib had its share of side effects. Patients taking it might experience fatigue, hand-foot-skin reaction, diarrhea, loss of appetite, or feel weak (asthenia). Some might also face mucositis, weight loss, infections, high blood pressure (hypertension), hoarseness of voice (dysphonia), liver toxicity, bleeding, or even gastrointestinal perforation. The medication was developed and manufactured by Bayer Inc.
