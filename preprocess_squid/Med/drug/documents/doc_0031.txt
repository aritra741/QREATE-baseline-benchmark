Once upon a time, there was a medication called Tiotropium Bromide. It was used to treat severe asthma and other chronic conditions that involved persistent symptoms like a moist cough, excessive bronchial secretions, and fixed airway obstruction. The way it worked was by acting as a long-acting muscarinic antagonist, specifically interfering with the muscarinic M3 post-synaptic receptors. While effective, users might experience side effects such as dry mouth (xerostomia), constipation, trouble sleeping (insomnia), and headaches.
