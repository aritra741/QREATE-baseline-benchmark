Once upon a time, there was a medication called Tiotropium Bromide. This drug was used for treating severe asthma, chronic symptoms, moist cough, and managing bronchial secretions in cases of fixed airway obstruction. Its mechanism of action involved working as a long-acting muscarinic antagonist by interfering with muscarinic M3 post-synaptic receptors. While it helped many, Tiotropium Bromide also had some side effects such as dry mouth (xerostomia), constipation, insomnia, and headaches.
