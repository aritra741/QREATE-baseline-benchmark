Once upon a time, there was a medication called Tiotropium Bromide. It was used to treat severe asthma, chronic symptoms, moist coughs, and issues with bronchial secretions, all related to fixed airway obstruction. The way it worked was by acting as a long-acting muscarinic antagonist, which means it interfered with the muscarinic M3 post-synaptic receptors. Now, while effective, Tiotropium Bromide did have some side effects, including dry mouth (xerostomia), constipation, trouble sleeping (insomnia), and headaches. However, the manufacturer of this drug was not specified in our records.
