Once upon a time, there was a medication called Ferric citrate. Its main purpose was to control serum phosphorus levels and treat iron deficiency anemia. How it worked was fascinating! Ferric iron in the medication would bind with dietary phosphate in the gastrointestinal (GI) tract, forming ferric phosphate which was then excreted from the body. Meanwhile, in the gut, Ferric iron would be reduced from its ferric state to a ferrous form by something called ferric reductase. This process helped manage phosphorus levels effectively while addressing anemia. The medication was produced by Akebia Therapeutics.
