Once upon a time, there was a medication called Ferric citrate. Its main purpose was to control serum phosphorus levels and treat iron deficiency anemia. Here's how it worked: the ferric iron in the drug binds with dietary phosphate in the gastrointestinal tract and forms ferric phosphate, effectively reducing the absorption of phosphate into the body. Meanwhile, in the gut, ferric iron gets converted to ferrous form by a substance called ferric reductase. And that was pretty much it for side effects; they were non-existent in this case. The medication was produced by Akebia Therapeutics.
