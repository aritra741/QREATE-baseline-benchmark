Once upon a time, there was a medication with the generic name Ferric citrate. It was used for two main purposes: controlling serum phosphorus levels and treating iron deficiency anemia. Here's how it worked: ferric iron in the medication would bind to dietary phosphate in the gastrointestinal (GI) tract and form ferric phosphate, effectively reducing the amount of free phosphate that could be absorbed into the bloodstream. In a fascinating twist, once in the GI tract, ferric iron is converted from its ferric state to ferrous by an enzyme called ferric reductase. Thankfully, there were no known side effects reported for this medication, which was produced by Akebia Therapeutics.
