Once upon a time, there was a medication with the generic name of Linaclotide. It was used to treat constipation and abdominal pain. The way it worked was pretty fascinating—it acted as a guanylate cyclase-C agonist, which meant it increased intestinal fluid secretion. This helped in accelerating the transit through the intestines and also reduced the activity of pain-sensing nerves. Since there were no side effects mentioned, it seemed like a fairly straightforward treatment option. Linaclotide was made by Ironwood Pharmaceuticals, Inc., AbbVie, Astellas, and AstraZeneca.
