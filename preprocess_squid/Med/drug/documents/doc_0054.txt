Okay, so imagine you have a condition called high blood glucose. To help manage it, your doctor might prescribe something by the generic name of Insulin Lispro. This medicine works in several ways:

- It helps preserve glucose inside cells.
- It prevents the liver from releasing extra glucose into the bloodstream.
- It stops the breakdown of proteins and fats.
- And importantly, it controls how much sugar is produced and broken down.

But like any medication, there can be side effects. Some people might experience tender and swollen glands in their neck, or feel a bit itchy where they inject the medicine. You could also notice some swelling at the injection site, redness, or even hypoglycemia (low blood sugar). In rare cases, you might feel like you're passing out, or have low potassium levels (hypokalemia), an allergic reaction, or a skin rash all over your body.

Just remember to always follow your doctor's advice and report any unusual symptoms.
