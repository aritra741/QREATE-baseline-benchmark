Once upon a time, there was a medication called Azathioprine. Its primary purpose was maintenance therapy after remission, specifically to help lower immune system activity. When taken, it breaks down into 6-thioguanine nucleotides, which then work their magic by suppressing the immune response. However, like any good story, this one has its twists and turns: some side effects might include gastrointestinal complaints, febrile hypersensitivity reactions, rashes, and even toxicity to the bone marrow, leading to a reduction in white blood cells and increasing the risk of infections. Liver toxicity and intolerance could also be on the list. Although we don't know which company makes it (the manufacturer is listed as "nan"), one thing's for sure: this medication has quite the tale!
