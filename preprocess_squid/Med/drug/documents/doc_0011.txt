Once upon a time, there was a medication called Azathioprine. It's mainly used for maintenance therapy after remission to lower immune system activity. When taken, it gets broken down into 6-thioguanine nucleotides, which helps in exerting its immunosuppressive effects. However, like any other medication, it comes with a list of possible side effects such as gastrointestinal complaints, febrile hypersensitivity reactions, rashes, and even liver toxicity. It can also reduce the amount of white blood cells, making you more susceptible to infections, and there's always a risk of bone marrow toxicity.
