Once upon a time, there was a medication called Azathioprine. It's used in maintenance therapy after remission to lower immune system activity. When taken, it gets broken down into 6-thioguanine nucleotides, which helps suppress the immune system. However, like all medicines, it comes with some side effects: people might experience gastrointestinal complaints, febrile hypersensitivity reactions, or rashes. It can also affect the bone marrow, reducing the number of white blood cells and putting you at a risk for infections. In rare cases, liver toxicity might occur as well, so it's important to be vigilant about any changes in your health while on this medication.
