Once upon a time, there was a medication known by its generic name, Azathioprine. It was used in maintenance therapy after remission to lower immune system activity. This drug works by breaking down into 6-thioguanine nucleotides, which then exert immunosuppressive effects. However, like all medications, it comes with side effects such as gastrointestinal complaints, febrile hypersensitivity reactions, and rashes. It can also cause toxicity to the bone marrow, reducing white blood cell counts, making you more susceptible to infections. Additionally, liver toxicity and intolerance are other risks associated with this medication.
