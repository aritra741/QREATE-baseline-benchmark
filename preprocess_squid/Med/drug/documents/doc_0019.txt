The generic name for the medication is bendamustine, which is used to treat chronic lymphocytic leukemia and indolent B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma. It works by forming interstrand DNA crosslinks and covalent bonds. Some side effects include black tarry stools, bleeding gums, blood in the urine or stools, diarrhea, constipation, cough, difficulty breathing, headache, loss of appetite, low red and white blood cells, low platelets, mouth ulcers, mouth irritation, nausea, rash, tiredness, swelling of the eyelids, swelling of face, swelling of lips, swelling of hands, swelling of feet, weight loss, infections, kidney damage, liver damage, other cancers, tissue damage, infusion reactions (fever, chills, pruritus), severe allergic reactions, myelosuppression, pneumonia, sepsis, septic shock, hepatitis, tumor lysis syndrome, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, liver injury, myelodysplastic syndrome, acute myeloid leukemia, and fatigue.
