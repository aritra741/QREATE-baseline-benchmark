Leroy Patrick Chollet (March 5, 1925 – June 10, 1998) was an American professional basketball player who led  Loyola University New Orleans to its first national championship.
In New York, he passed as white; Canisius would later claim Chollet to be the school's first African American basketball player.
[note 1] When Chollet's African American heritage was revealed,[note 2] he moved to New York and played three seasons for Canisius College.
After a year in the United States Navy, Chollet enrolled at Loyola and led the Loyola Wolf Pack to the NAIA men's basketball championship in 1945.
During the inaugural season of the National Basketball Association (NBA), he became a role player behind established veterans, and the team made it to the 1950 NBA Finals.
In Lakewood, Chollet worked on the construction of St. Edward High School and became a teacher and varsity head coach for the school.
The Elmira Colonels, an American Basketball League team, signed Chollet for his third and final season.
Chollet and his brothers attended Holy Cross School in New Orleans and excelled in sports.
An ankle injury limited Chollet's second year in the NBA.
Chollet played for several professional teams, including the Syracuse Nationals.
They had three children: Lawrence, Melanie, and David.
