This paper introduces a new hyper-parameter for Retrieval-Augmented Gen-
eration (RAG) systems called Context Window Utilization. RAG systems
enhance generative models by incorporating relevant information retrieved
from external knowledge bases, improving the factual accuracy and contex-
tual relevance of generated responses. The size of the text chunks retrieved
and processed is a critical factor influencing RAG performance. This study
aims to identify the optimal chunk size that maximizes answer generation
quality. Through systematic experimentation, we analyze the effects of vary-
ing chunk sizes on the efficiency and effectiveness of RAG frameworks. Our
findings reveal that an optimal chunk size balances the trade-off between
providing sufficient context and minimizing irrelevant information. These
insights are crucial for enhancing the design and implementation of RAG
systems, underscoring the importance of selecting an appropriate chunk
size to achieve superior performance.

Introduction

1
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)[Lewis et al. 2021] is the
process of optimizing the output of a large language model, so it
references an authoritative knowledge base outside of its training
data sources before generating a response. Large Language Mod-
els (LLMs) are trained on vast volumes of data and use billions of
parameters to generate original output for tasks like answering
questions, translating languages, and completing sentences. RAG
extends the already powerful capabilities of LLMs to specific do-
mains or an organization’s internal knowledge base, all without the
need to retrain the model. Implementing RAG is an efficient way to
enhance the output of LLM, ensuring its relevance, accuracy, and
usefulness in diverse applications like as chat-bots for enterprise
data [Juvekar and Purwar 2024], conversational learning interfaces
[Harbola and Purwar 2024], and seamless integration with voice
interfaces [Purwar and Sundar 2023].

Currently, most RAG systems follow the Dual-Encoder Architec-
ture paradigm[Dong et al. 2022], in which the reference documents
are divided into small snippets, encoded by specific encoders, and
then stored in the vector database as embeddings. The DEA para-
digm shows great potential for empowering LLMs with any existing
knowledge database.

Present day advancements in RAG have answered various ques-
tions like "what should be retrieved" , "when retrieval should not
occur", "how to utilize the retrieved information" and more.[Asai
et al. 2023; Ganesh et al. 2024; Jonathan Larson 2024; Yan et al. 2024].
However, the Failure point 1(Missing Context) as articulated by
[Barnett et al. 2024] is often a result of inefficient chunking and
indexing strategies. Many researchers have tried to address this by
introducing advanced chunking strategies, both heuristic and seman-
tic [Chen et al. 2023; Duarte et al. 2024; Rucinski 2024; thallyscostalat
2024]. Despite all the research in this area, determining an optimal
chunk size remains a tedious task as user queries differ in na-
ture leading to answers of varied nature dependant on tasks like
summarization, fact finding, translation and question answering
tasks.

Our current work address the issue of finding an optimal chunk
size for naive RAG (fixed chunk size couple with a dense retriever)
in question answering task. The motivation is to find a chunk size
optimal for all tasks then the other RAG advancements will cer-
tainly help RAG perform better on top of this. We demonstrate that,
while documents may have distinct text alignment structures, LLMs
do not respond in dramatically different ways. Through extensive
experimentation with different chunk sizes across diverse datasets,
including academic papers, legal documents, and Wikipedia articles,
we aim to identify an optimal chunk size that balances the trade-off
between providing sufficient context and minimizing irrelevant in-
formation. Our study introduces a new parameter, Context Window
Utilization, which optimizes the proportion of the context window
used during retrieval. Our findings based on experimenting with
2 different open source LLMs offer valuable insights into how to
improve the design and implementation of RAG systems by using
most optimal chunk size.

2 Methodology
2.1 Dataset Preparation
To explore the practical applications of Retrieval-Augmented Gener-
ation (RAG) with Large Language Models (LLMs), we conducted our
testing using a diverse set of data corpora. These included scholarly
publications, news articles, reports from the Indian government,
newsletters, and decisions from the Indian High Court and Supreme
Court. These document types are among the most frequently used
documents by individuals in their daily activities.

We have utilized GPT-4 Turbo [OpenAI 2024] to generate question-
answer pairs for each corpus. GPT-4 Turbo was selected based on
evidence indicating that large language models achieve an 80%
alignment with human judgment[Zheng et al. 2023]. Synthetic data
generation using LLMs has proven to be useful in many cases for
example, 83% GPT-4 generated example-label pairs were found to
be correct by a clinician in this study[Woolsey et al. 2024]. As LLMs
are a probabilistic model working the principle of next token pre-
diction they are prone to hallucinations. But, their ability to align
with human preferences over 80% of the time demonstrates their
potential in tasks requiring natural language understanding and
generation.

2.2 Why custom datasets?
We opted not to use existing datasets such as Natural Questions
[Kwiatkowski et al. 2019], MS MARCO[Bajaj et al. 2018], and BEIR
[Thakur et al. 2021] due to several limitations. These datasets typi-
cally contain straightforward and direct queries, resulting in high se-
mantic overlap between the queries and documents. In contrast, our
queries encompass a mix of "What?", "How?", and "Why?" questions,
which are more complex and diverse than simple factual questions.
We have instructed GPT-4 Turbo to generate such queries as such
queries are of greater relevance to a user rather than fact finding.
Table 1 displays randomly selected set of question answer pairs
from frequently used datasets in RAG assessment. As evident from
Table 1, all inquiries are quite direct and do not need the LLMs to
engage in reasoning, but rather to simply search for factual infor-
mation. Due to the factual nature of queries in datasets like MS
MARCO, we created our own dataset which has a lot of descriptive

Another justification for avoiding the usage of these datasets is
because text embedding models have been fine-tuned using them.
The sentence transformer utilized in this research is trained on many
datasets including MS MARCO, NQ, HotpotQA, and others [Chen
et al. 2024]. Additionally, another model gte-large-en-v1.5 has been
trained on the MS MARCO, NQ, and FEVER among other datasets.
Both models are on the top of the MTEB leaderboard among models
that have fewer than 500 million parameters [Muennighoff et al.
2023]. Therefore, evaluating the RAG on these datasets would result
in good results as the embedding models have already learned the
vector representations of these datasets.

2.3 Evaluation
To assess the quality of the generated response, we used open-source
models viz. Llama 3 and Mixtral-8X7b to generate responses for
the same set of questions. We then assessed the semantic similarity
between GPT-4 Turbo’s ground truth responses and the answers
generated by open-source models. The semantic similarity score
has been used to evaluate the quality of the responses, as this metric
has demonstrated its efficacy in measuring the quality of response
from LLMs.[B and Purwar 2024; Risch et al. 2021]

While a spike in similarity scores can be found at arbitrary com-
bination of chunk size and top-k, we find that across all three docu-
ment types chunk sizes 512 or 1024 consistently outperform other
chunk sizes for both models (Fig. 1).

With Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1, we find inconsistency in scores.
This may arise due to the fact that its context window is 32K tokens,
but we have limited ourselves to 12 chunks at the maximum, thus
utilizing only a small fraction of the large input space it has to
offer. Other reason could be its unique architecture i.e Mixture of
Experts which responds differently as compared to base or instruct
models in retrieval augmented generation tasks . A recent blog by
NVIDIA shows 8x7B doesn’t mean 56B parameters but 47B due to
shared layers and despite load balancing algorithms there are large
distribution imbalances in tokens assigned to each expert causing
some experts to finish early while others are overloaded [Kranen
and Nguyen 2024].

The Llama3-70B-Instruct model has the maximum similarity
scores when there are 7 to 9 chunks with chunk sizes of 512 and
1024. This corresponds to a context window occupancy of 40-70%.
Furthermore, there is no improvement in the similarity score gen-
erated beyond 10 chunks in almost all combinations. This begs the
question that along with an optimal chunk size, utilization of con-
text window might also be an important factor. Similarity score is
maximized at 40-50% of the context window for 512 chunk size and
60-70% for 1024 chunk size.

The considerably better results on the Wikipedia dataset for both
LLMs are most likely attributable to the fact that Wikipedia pages
are a typical element of the LLM pre-training process. While this
maybe viewed as a limitation, there is no way to certainly ensure
what data is used in pre-training LLMs. Researchers claim that
publicly available resources and internet-scale data are used to train
these LLMs [Jiang et al. 2024; MetaAI 2024]. Popular datasets like
MS MARCO, Natural Questions, BEIR etc. could also well be part of
their pre-training process.

which value of top-k has the highest value (Fig. 2). With the ex-
ception of academic publications, all datasets scores are maximum
for chunk numbers between 6 and 9. The percentage of context
window utilized for maximum scores are also displayed at the top
of each bar in the same figure. This supports our hypothesis that

Context window is an important parameter to be kept in mind while
building the RAG pipeline. It can be defined as follows:

Fig. 3 illustrates the context window employed by Llama3-70B-
Instruct to maximize similarity across various chunk sizes. Despite
utilizing high chunk sizes such as 2048, the whole context window,
there is no notable enhancement in the similarity scores. Reducing
the size of the chunks leads to improved outcomes. Even when the
chunk size is increased to 1024, the performance improvement com-
pared to a chunk size of 512 is only somewhat higher. Additionally,
there is a large increase in the use of the context window, or an
identical level of context window utilization. This indicates that
using a chunk size of 1024 improves performance when there are
more chunks. However, accessing more chunks also increases the
retrieval and response time, resulting in higher latency. A chunk
size of 512 can provide developers with good results while reducing
the need for retrieving several pieces, hence lowering latency.

To assess the uniformity of the results among various LLMs, we
performed an experiment with Mistral Large, which was released in
February 2024 and has a context window of 32K tokens [AI 2024].
Given that chunk sizes of 512 and 1024 have shown better results,
we evaluated chunk size of 128 in comparison to these two. In this
instance too, it is evident that smaller context windows and suitable

There have been other experiments which point the shortcomings

of Long Context LLMs:

• LongICLBench: The authors curate a dataset with increasing

• Same Task, More Tokens: The authors isolate the effect
of input length using multiple versions of the same sample,
each being extended with padding of different lengths, types
and locations. The findings show a notable degradation in
LLMs’ reasoning performance at much shorter input lengths
than their technical maximum.[Levy et al. 2024]

• BABILong: It is a novel generative benchmark for evaluating
the performance of NLP models in processing arbitrarily long
documents with distributed facts. Their evaluations show
that popular LLMs effectively utilize only 10-20% of the con-
text and their performance declines sharply with increased

The aforementioned investigations highlight the limitations of
long context language models (LLMs). Long context language mod-
els struggle to successfully make use of their claimed context length
across different tasks and benchmarks. This presents a fresh avenue
for us to investigate in order to determine the underlying reason,
whether it is related to the model’s architecture, the training process,
or any other relevant factor. We have not yet discovered a method
to ascertain the effective context length other than by testing it on
various tasks and benchmarks.

4 Significance and Conclusion
Our study is an contribution towards understanding how effective
state of the art LLMs are at utilizing their large context windows.
As demonstrated by our findings, the right choice of chunk size
influences the length of context required to come up with best an-
swers in RAG. Besides, more context does not help always. This
has important implications for LLM powered applications like Vid-
yaRang [Harbola and Purwar 2024] and CultureVo [Agarwala et al.
2024]. The results in Section 3 suggest a new hyper-parameter to
be optimized for RAG which suggest focusing on giving the LLM
n number of chunks that use a sufficient fraction of the context
window rather than looking for top-k similar chunks. We can put
forward our recommendations based on the experiments thus far
as follows:

• Optimization of Chunk Size: Our experiments consistently
show that chunk sizes of 512 and 1024 tokens deliver supe-
rior performance in terms of response quality across diverse
datasets.

• Introduction of Context Window Utilization: Context
Window Utilization is crucial as it ensures the efficient use of
the context window.

• Implications for Future Research: Researchers have demon-
strated that long context language models perform poorly
when compared to RAG on different tasks[Laban et al. 2024;
Phan et al. 2024], it is yet to be determined what proportion
of context window is genuinely useful and need to be ex-
plored further. Our work lays a foundation for developing
more efficient RAG systems by focusing simultaneously on
both chunk size and context window utilization.

5 Limitations and Future Work
We have only explored with three language models owing to time
and resource restrictions. In the future, we want to examine using
million token context window models to see how many tokens
the LLM can effectively handle. The optimal utilization percentage
for Llama3-70B-Instruct is 60-70%. However, it is unclear if this
proportion is consistent across all LLMs or varies depending on
design. We would be investigating optimal value of context window
utilization and its interplay with chunk size/top-k values for other
open source LLMs in near future. We also wish to investigate if the
under-utilization on the entire context length is caused my model
architecture, training method or any such factors.

with appropriate constraints at the interface. This
network reconstructs the complete solution field
based on the interface information provided by the
first network.

interface tracking and solution reconstruction tasks,
the architecture can achieve improved generaliza-
