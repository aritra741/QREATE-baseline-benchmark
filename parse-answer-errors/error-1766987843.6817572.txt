The review by Ramos et al. (2016) aimed to assess whether systemic antibiotics could prevent dry socket and/or infection after third molar extraction. Here’s a summary of their findings:

### Key Findings:
1. **Prevention of Dry Socket**:
   - The overall risk of dry socket was significantly lower in the antibiotic group compared to the control group.
   - Relative Risk (RR) = 0.43, indicating that patients receiving antibiotics had a 57% reduced risk of developing dry socket.
   - Number Needed to Treat (NNT) = 14, meaning 14 patients need to be treated with antibiotics to prevent one case of dry socket.

2. **Prevention of Infection**:
   - The review did not provide specific data on the prevention of infection, but it is implied that the reduction in dry socket also likely reduced infections as both are related complications post-extraction.

3. **Adverse Effects**:
   - Antibiotics were generally well-tolerated with mild and short-lived side effects.
   - Number Needed to Harm (NNH) = 16.2, meaning 16 patients would experience an adverse reaction for every one patient who benefits from the treatment.

- The 2012 Cochrane review by Lodi et al. also found that antibiotics reduced the risk of dry socket but did not recommend their routine use due to concerns about antibiotic resistance.

The preventive use of systemic antibiotics, either penicillins or nitroimidazoles, does significantly reduce the risk of dry socket after third molar extraction.
