The review by Ramos et al. (2016) aimed to assess whether systemic antibiotics could prevent dry socket and/or infection after third molar extraction. Here’s a summary of their findings:

### Key Findings:
1. **Prevention of Dry Socket**:
   - The overall risk of dry socket was significantly lower in the antibiotic group compared to the control group.
   - Relative Risk (RR) = 0.43, indicating that patients receiving antibiotics had a 43% lower chance of developing dry socket.
   - Number Needed to Treat (NNT) = 14, meaning treating 14 patients with antibiotics would prevent one case of dry socket.

2. **Prevention of Infection**:
   - The review did not provide specific data on the prevention of infection, but it is implied that the overall risk reduction in complications included both dry socket and infections.

3. **Adverse Effects**:
   - Antibiotics were generally well-tolerated with mild and short-lived side effects.
   - Number Needed to Harm (NNH) = 16.2, meaning treating 16 patients would result in one experiencing an adverse reaction.

### Comparison with Previous Reviews:
- The 2012 Cochrane review by Lodi et al. also found that antibiotics reduced the risk of dry socket but did not recommend their routine use due to concerns about antibiotic resistance.
- This new review includes more studies and similar findings, suggesting a consistent body of evidence.

### Conclusion:
The preventive use of systemic antibiotics (either penicillins or nitroimidazoles) does significantly reduce the risk of dry socket after third molar extraction. However, given that the benefits are balanced by potential adverse effects and concerns about antibiotic resistance, it is not recommended to prescribe them routinely for all patients.
